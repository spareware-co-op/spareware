# CON-A4-F4-P3: A simple roof (first half)

**Status, 2026-11-23:** Decided for the build (piece A63, the data and shape half of job 5.3). The crate `construct-roofs` follows it and its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-roofs/FORMAT.md) is the stored form; construct-regen's `roofs` fills a roof's recipe, its `roof_plan` reads the filled recipe for both builders, and its `kernel` module's child `roof` builds it through the engine seam. The commands, the server's view of a roof and the page's Roof tool are the next lanes'.

## The question

A roof is the third system element with layers, after the wall and the floor. Job 5.3 asks for the simple ones: a roof made from a footprint with a slope set on each edge, flat, mono-pitch or gable, with an eaves overhang, a layered type, on a level, built as solids. Before commands and pages could make roofs, this half had to settle how a slope is held, which footprints each form takes, how the layers follow the slope, where every corner lands on the kernel's grid, and what a roof measures exactly.

## How roofs are built and drawn

A pitched roof bears on the walls along its eaves: its rafters (or a slab) sit on the wall plate at the wall line, rise at the roof's pitch, run on past the wall as the overhang, and are cut off plumb (upright) at the fascia. A gable's two slopes rise from opposite walls to a ridge; its other two sides end at the gable walls, run on past them as the verges. A roof's layers (the covering, battens or an underlay, the rafters or deck, insulation, a lining) each have a thickness measured square to the slope: a rafter's depth is across its length, a tile's or a board's thickness through it, and a section through a pitched roof draws every layer that thick at right angles to the slope. Pitch is given either way in common practice: as a rise over a run (1 in 4, 25%) or as an angle (30 degrees); New Zealand's acceptable solution for external moisture, E2/AS1, for one, gives minimum roof pitches in degrees.

## The decisions

- **The footprint is a floor's boundary.** construct-slabs' `Boundary` (one loop of straight edges in grid steps, every edge with its permanent ID, checked by construct-slabs' own code) is a roof's footprint, unchanged: it is the line the roof bears on. A roof's footprint has no holes yet (an opening through a roof is job 4.2's).
- **Each edge has an overhang and, if the roof rises from it, a pitch.** The overhang is how far the roof runs on past the edge in plan, 0 or more. An edge the roof is not given has neither.
- **A pitch is held as it was given**, in construct-params' own values: a rise over a run (`Slope`, two whole numbers in lowest terms, exact) or an angle (`Angle`, the kernel's angle units, exact as typed). Which form a person gave is primary, as the geometry kernel holds for bearings (its chapter 17).
- **The shape is worked from a slope.** A slope is used as it is. An angle is worked as the slope whose run is 10^9 and whose rise is the nearest whole number to 10^9 times its tangent: 30 degrees is 577,350,269 in 1,000,000,000. The tangent is the one float in the crate, computed by libm (a pure Rust port of musl's, so the same bits on every machine), rounded to a whole rise at once and never stored. It is exact enough: the slope is within 5 x 10^-10 of the true tangent, so a height worked from it is off the true height by at most 5 x 10^-10 times its run, 0.000025 mm over 50 m, four hundred times finer than the model's tolerance of 0.01 mm. Whole thousandths of a degree were not chosen as the store, since a slope typed as a ratio (1 in 4) has no exact angle and a typed angle need not be whole thousandths.
- **A pitch rises and is no steeper than 10 in 1** (about 84.3 degrees): a slope of 0 or less is refused (an edge that does not slope is given no pitch), and so is a steeper one, or an angle of 90 degrees or more.
- **The form is worked out from which edges have a pitch**, and nothing else is a roof here:
  - none: **flat**, on any footprint construct-slabs takes;
  - one: **mono-pitch**, rising from that edge (its eaves) across to the opposite edge (its high side);
  - two opposite edges at one slope: **gable**, rising from both to a ridge midway between them.
- **Refused, each naming its edges and the job that lifts it:** two edges that meet at a corner, three, or all four (hips: "every edge slopes: hips come with job 6.2"); two opposite edges at different slopes (an uneven gable, whose ridge is solved with job 6.2); a pitch on any footprint other than a rectangle whose edges run along the plan's x and y axes (job 6.2 solves every footprint); an overhang on a flat footprint with an edge off the axes (job 6.2); a footprint with a hole (job 4.2).
- **Which footprints each form takes, exactly:** flat, any footprint construct-slabs takes (straight edges, no holes); with overhangs, one whose every edge runs along x or y, each edge moved out square to itself by its overhang and the corners where the moved edges meet, so every corner is a grid point; two edges on one line take one overhang, and overhangs that fold the outline back are refused. Mono-pitch and gable: a rectangle along the plan's axes, any size, from any of its edges. A gable over an L, a T or a rotated rectangle is refused.
- **The base and the frame.** The roof's bottom face crosses every footprint edge at its base, its level's elevation plus its offset (construct-levels' tie, as every element's), so its shape has `z = 0` there and its layers stand on the bottom face; the built row holds the base's height, so a moved level moves the roof through the regenerator's `reads` table.
- **The layers follow the slope square to it.** A layer's thickness is measured square to its faces, as roofs are built and drawn. On a slope `s`, a face `h` square to the bottom face stands `h * sqrt(1 + s^2)` above it measured upright; that height is rounded to the kernel's grid once per face, the nearest step a half up, by exact integer square roots (no float). Every corner of a face moves up by that one whole number of steps, so two layers share their face bit for bit and every face is exactly parallel to the bottom face, as a layered wall's faces are (its `wall_plan` rule). On a flat roof `h` is the height itself.
- **Every corner is rounded once.** In the frame of the eaves edge (`a` across the footprint, `b` along the edge), the eaves are cut plumb at `a = -overhang`, the high side or far eaves at `W + overhang`, the verges at `b = -overhang` and `L + overhang`. A gable's ridge is at `a = W / 2`, its height the nearest step to `s W / 2`; each eaves corner's height is the nearest step to the plane's height there. Those are the only roundings, each once, and rounding a half up moves with a whole number added, so every face's corners are those corners moved up by the face's whole height and a gable's two slopes meet at the ridge exactly in every face. A wall or plan corner on the hundredth of a millimetre never moves in plan.
- **Each layer is one solid on the kernel's grid,** the solid construct-roofs works out and the builder asks the engine for: a flat layer is its eaves outline extruded straight up from its lower face to its upper face; a sloped layer is its section across the roof (a parallelogram, or a gable's six-sided chevron) at one verge, lofted to the same section at the other (`Engine::loft`, every wall of which is flat). A layer of no thickness (a membrane) is a face between its neighbours, with no solid. No method was added to the engine trait.
- **Measures are exact:** the plan area is the eaves outline's, overhangs included; the slope area is the top face as laid, the plan area times `sqrt(1 + s^2)` to the nearest square step; each layer's volume is its solid's, exactly, from its grid corners (an area or volume between two whole steps is refused, never rounded: typed lengths never meet it); mass from the material's density where the project's materials hold one. A sloped roof reads back its pitch as given and a gable its ridge height (its top face along the ridge above its base). They are read-only parameters (`roof.area`, `roof.slope-area`, `roof.thickness`, `roof.volume`, `roof.mass`, `roof.pitch`, `roof.ridge-height`, and `roof.layer-<k>.volume` and `.mass`), worked out each time and never stored, shown by the server's parameters view after a roof's category's own, as a floor's are.
- **A roof type is a floor type's layers in a block of its own:** of the roofs category, its layers from the top (outer) face down in its `@system.roof` block, spelt exactly as a floor type's `@system.floor` text (construct-slabs' `Layers::to_text`).
- **Shared, not copied, and not moved:** the footprint and the layers are construct-slabs' own types and checks. They were not moved to a common crate: construct-slabs reads well as their home while floors and roofs are its only users, and the walls' layers are their own crate's (kept equal to a floor's by a test). Main may want one crate for the layered systems' layers (walls, floors, roofs, later ceilings) when a third reader of the same stack comes; the move changes no stored byte.
- **The recipe** holds the stored footprint, the edges (every footprint edge in the footprint's order, its overhang and its pitch in construct-params' spellings) and `{"type":"layers"}`; the regenerator fills it from the roof's type before it is built, into the form both builders take: the form, the edges it rises from, the eaves outline and each layer with its solid, in grid steps. A roof's `reads` row lists the parameter table and its type, so a change to its type's layers rebuilds every roof of it.
- **Names:** a flat roof's faces are `top`, `bottom`, `face.<k>` between layers and `side.<edge ID>`; a sloped roof names each slope by the edge it rises from, `top.<edge>`, `bottom.<edge>` and `face.<k>.<edge>`, and every edge's eaves, high side or verge `side.<edge ID>`. Edge IDs, not positions, name them, so a roof drawn again with the same edges keeps its faces' names.
- **The stand-in builds the box around the layers**, its displays drawing each sloped layer as its loft, each point the nearest millimetre, and a flat roof's layers as the box's slices, so the default setting builds every model with roofs and a page shows its slopes. Each layer is a part of the displays on both builders, with its material and function.
- **Default roof types:** sheet steel (0.55 mm) on an underlay on 150 mm timber rafters, and 100 mm foam glass insulation on a 200 mm concrete slab. Every material is one of construct-materials' defaults with a published density, so a default roof's mass is known; the rafters are one solid-timber layer, so their mass is overstated, and said so.

## Why

- A roof reads its type as a wall and a floor do, through one argument the regenerator fills, so a type edit rebuilds every roof and a command and a build cannot read a roof differently.
- The bottom face at the wall line is how a roof is set out: its rafters bear on the wall plate, and the overhang drops below it.
- Holding a pitch as given keeps what a person typed exactly, and working it as one exact slope gives every machine the same corners.
- Square-to-the-slope layers are what a roof's makers and its drawings mean by a layer's thickness; one rounding per face keeps the faces parallel and shared, as the wall's rule does.
- Lofting a sloped layer between its verges gives every solid flat faces through grid corners, which the engine makes exactly.

## Not decided here

- Hips, valleys, uneven gables and sloped roofs on any footprint (job 6.2, roof solving, CON-A4-F4-P4); dormers (CON-A4-F3-P4); soffits, fascias and gutters (CON-A4-F4-P3, its later half).
- Openings through a roof (job 4.2).
- The commands (`roof.create` and the rest, each one entry and one undo step, and the generic commands refused behind them, as the floor's are), the server's read-only view through the commands, the page's Roof tool and the two-browser section: the next lanes'.
- Eaves cut square to the slope instead of plumb, and a roof's bottom face set at another reference (its top, or a structure layer's face) than the wall line: later versions of the recipe.
