# CON-A4-F4-P1: A floor's slope, slab edges and sketched outline

**Status, 2026-11-24:** Decided for the build (piece A72, job 5.2, the shape half). construct-slabs' `boundary` solves a sketched outline and its `shape` holds a slope arrow and slab edges; the stored form is in its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-slabs/FORMAT.md); construct-regen's `floor_plan` reads the filled recipe for both builders and its `kernel` child `floor` builds it through the engine seam. Commands, the page and the two-browser test are the next job's.

## The question

The first floor (job 4.3, [the floor note](CON-A4-F4-P1-floor-from-boundary.md)) is a flat layered slab from typed corners. A floor also falls to a drain or a ramp, thickens at its edges where it bears on the ground or carries a downstand beam, and is drawn as a sketch whose dimensions drive it. Job 5.2 had to settle how each is held exactly on the length grid, what each reads back, and what is refused.

## How floors slope and thicken

A floor slopes by one plane: a direction in plan and a rise along it, given as two heights at the ends of an arrow or as a fall such as 1 in 60. Its layers keep their thickness, the plane's normal tilting only slightly at floor slopes. At its edges a ground slab is made deeper, often with a tapered inner face, and a suspended slab may carry a downstand along a side; both run the side's length and meet at corners.

## The decisions

- **A sketched outline is the solved sketch.** A floor's sketch may hold construct-solver's constraints. The floor's corners are the sketch as `solve` gives it (the solver family recipes use, from the drawn points, default budget); the stored sketch keeps the drawn points, so a stored boundary always solves to the same corners, and a sketch with no constraints is its own solution, so every floor stored before is unchanged. A dimension change re-solves from the corners the floor has, with the new value. The checks of job 4.3 run on the solved corners, so an outline a dimension change makes cross itself is refused as a drawn one is; a sketch the solver does not solve is refused with the solver's reason. Allowing constraints took a check away and changed no stored byte, as the floor note planned.
- **One slope arrow**, from a tail to a head in plan, with the top face's height at the tail and either the height at the head or a rise over a run along the arrow (construct-params' `Slope`). Heights are from the floor's tied height. The plane is level across the arrow.
- **Exact on the grid, or refused.** The height at a corner is a ratio of whole numbers; the slope is taken only when every corner of every loop lands on a whole grid step, otherwise it is refused naming the corner's edge. The layers are then the floor's outer loop lifted onto the plane (grid points on one plane) and lofted to the same loop moved straight up by the layer's thickness, each hole cut through by its own loft, so no face is approximated (the engine answers an extrusion of a profile that is not level with a curved surface, so a sloped layer is not asked for as one). A rise over a run needs an arrow of whole-step length (along x or y, or a whole length such as 3, 4, 5), since along any other the arrow's length is a root. Corners and heights in whole millimetres along x or y always land: the grid has 6,400 steps to the millimetre.
- **Plumb thickness.** Each layer hangs straight down from the top face by its type's thickness. The side faces stay upright, the volume is the plan area times the thickness exactly, and the top and bottom faces are parallel. Measured square to the slope the thickness is the plumb one times the cosine of the slope (0.99995 at 1 in 100); a type thickness held square to a steep slope would put every corner at a root, so it is not offered.
- **The sloped top area is held to the nearest square step.** It is the plan area times the root of one plus the gradient squared: exact only where that root is whole in ratio (an arrow of 4 rising 3 gives 5/4), otherwise the nearest whole square step, worked in num-bigint's integers (never a float), with a flag saying which. The highest and lowest points are corners of the outer loop (a plane's extremes over a polygon are at corners and holes lie inside the outer loop).
- **Slab edges are pieces of their own** along chosen edges of the boundary (outer loop or hole): a thickened edge (a width at the underside tapering to a foot, a depth below the floor) or a downturn (an upright width and depth), flush with the floor's side face. Each piece is its plan at the underside and at its foot; its volume is exact by the prismatoid rule (all its corners lie on two parallel planes and every face is flat), twelve times which is a whole number, and refused when the volume is not a whole number of cubic steps; its length is its edge's.
- **Corners:** two sides with the same profile are mitred on the corner's bisector, so a thickening all round is one continuous ring; a side next to a plain one ends square; different profiles meeting are refused (a mitre between two sections is not one plane), and so is a square end at a corner sharper than a right angle (it would stand outside the floor). Every corner of a piece must be a grid point; across an edge not along x or y that holds only where the edge's length divides the width, and is refused by name otherwise. A piece reaching another side of the floor, or two overlapping (exact convex separation on the grid), is refused.
- **Joined by the engine's flat-faced union.** The builder unites each piece with the floor's lowest layer of any thickness, whose material and part it takes. A union the engine does not answer is its refusal; a floor is never built without its edges.
- **Refused together:** a slope and slab edges on one floor (the pieces would follow the sloped underside: their corners would not be on one level plane). It is refused by name until a later version builds it.
- **Versions.** A floor with a slope or slab edges is recipe version 2, its `slope` and `edges` stored beside the boundary; a floor with neither stays version 1 byte for byte. A reader that knows only version 1 refuses a shaped floor by its version, never building it flat. The filled recipe carries each corner's top height and each piece's two plans, so the builders read numbers and work out nothing.
- **What it reads back:** a sloped floor's top face area, highest and lowest points; each slab edge's length and volume. The floor's area, thickness and volume stay the slab's, so a schedule of floor volumes does not change meaning; edges are counted apart. A flat floor without edges has none of the new rows.
- **The stand-in** builds the box around a shaped floor (from the bottom face's lowest corner or the deepest foot to the top face's highest corner) and says it strays by the box's whole diagonal; the kernel's build replaces it, as for every stand-in shape.

## Not here
