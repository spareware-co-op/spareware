# CON-A4-F4-P2: Ceilings

**Status, 2026-11-29:** Decided for the build (piece A101, job 6.8). The data half is in `construct-slabs` (`ceiling`, `ceiling_type`, `ceiling_measure`, `tiles`; stored form in its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-slabs/FORMAT.md)), the commands in construct-api's `cloud::ceilings`, the room's upper bound in construct-regen's `rooms`, the page in `app/tools/architecture/ceiling.js`.

## The question

A ceiling is what a person sees over a room: a layered finish with a height from its level, placed by clicking inside the room or by sketching an outline, with a grid of tiles where it is a suspended ceiling. Rooms also want it as their upper bound.

## The decisions

- **A ceiling is a floor's slab under its own kind.** Its record kind and its recipe's operation are `ceiling`, its category the ceilings category, its recipe a floor's version 1 (boundary and the argument that reads its type's layers) with an optional `grid`. Both builders read it as a floor (`floor_plan::is_floor` accepts both operations), so no second builder exists and a ceiling is cut by openings and shafts as a floor is. It never slopes and never has slab edges.
- **Its tie is its exposed face.** A person types "2700 above Level 1" meaning the underside, so the tie is the underside and the layers stand above it. The builders put a slab's upper face at its element's elevation, so `levels::placed` lifts a ceiling's elevation by its type's total thickness (`Placed::rise`, `Placed::underside`): one place, read by every consumer. A change to the type's layers builds the ceilings of it again, through the `reads` row a floor has.
- **Layers run from the upper face down**, as a floor type's do; the last layer is the finish a person sees.
- **Placed by a click, outlined by the room search.** `ceiling.create` with `at` finds the loop of walls round the point on the ceiling's level with `construct_rooms::find` over the walls' outlines as joined (the room's own faces), and writes it as an ordinary boundary: it does not follow the walls afterwards (a warning says when the walls no longer enclose it). A sketched outline is the floor's boundary, its holes the ceiling's openings.
- **The grid** is a tile `width` and `depth`, an `origin` and an `angle` in whole degrees. Its tiles are counted exactly in whole numbers: a tile is whole when no edge of the outline crosses its open interior and its centre is in the region, cut when an edge does. **Interim:** only 0 and 90 degrees are counted; a turned grid's lines are a sine and a cosine away from the length grid's rationals, so its count waits for the kernel's exact rotation of a plane pattern (the same tests judge it). It is kept and drawn meanwhile, and `Grid::tiles` refuses it by name.
- **A room is bounded above by the ceiling over it.** The lowest ceiling of the room's level whose outline holds the room's point (not on an edge, not in an opening) and whose exposed face is above the room's base lowers the room's height where it is below the room's own limit; the room's volume is its area times that height and its built shape reaches it. A ceiling made, changed or deleted builds the rooms of its level again (`is_spatial`). Ceilings bound rooms by default; plan job 6.3b makes that switchable on every element.
- **Warning:** `ceiling.not_enclosed` when the walls of the ceiling's level close no loop round a point inside its outline (a point found by the first ear of the outline that lies in the region, the same for the same outline).
- **Schedule:** a Ceilings schedule lists the area, perimeter, thickness, volume, mass and tiles (the product's own read-only rows, `construct-schedules`' `QUANTITIES`).
- **Not here:** a sloped ceiling. The floors' slope arrow slopes the slab's top face and hangs the layers plumb under it, while a ceiling's height is its exposed face; a sloped ceiling is its own piece, with the room volume under it (construct-rooms refuses a sloped limit by name). A reflected ceiling plan drawn by the server (the plan's drawing is a floor plan's): the page draws ceilings in its plan view and in 3D.
