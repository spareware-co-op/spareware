# CON-A8-F2-P1: The drawing types, and the cut of a plan

**Status, 2026-11-28:** Sections and elevations added (piece A85, job 8.1, [below](#sections-and-elevations-piece-a85)): their cut, the outlines of what they see beyond, and their levels and grids. **Status, 2026-11-23:** Decided for the build: note [A12](A12-hidden-lines.md)'s build-order steps 2 and 3 (piece A48) and step 4, region merge and hatch (piece A58, [below](#step-4-regions-merged-by-material-and-hatched)). Code: `crates/construct-drawing`, the kernel seam's `Engine::section` (`geom-op`'s engine module) answered by geom-solidcore's engine, and `Kernel::solid` and `Kernel::section` in construct-regen. All of it is interim: the kernel's own `draw` (chapter 18 of the geometry architecture, `geom-draw`, pieces G387, G389 and G394 to G402) and its 2D booleans and hatches (`geom-2d`, G196, G199, G200, G202 and G210) replace it under the same tests. **Next:** a plan taken from the server's stored model and shown in the page (A12 step 4's output reaching people), then edges seen below the cut (step 5).

## The question

Note A12 chose the method and the order of the work. Before any line can be drawn, Spare Construct needs a drawing as plain values that the kernel's engine can produce one day unchanged, and the first drawing: what a plan's plane cuts. Four things were open: how a view maps model points to drawing points exactly; how a line names where it came from when the engine seam carries no persistent names; what the seam's cut takes and gives; and which engine answers it today.

## The decision

- **Views along grid axes only,** as A12 limits the first engine: a plan looks down, sections and elevations look north, south, east or west. A view has three whole unit vectors, the direction looked (`v`), up the sheet and right (`v × up`); a model point `p` in grid steps is the drawing point `(right · p, up · p)` in the same steps, exactly. The cut plane is square to `v` with its normal at the viewer. The table is in `construct-drawing/src/view.rs`.
- **Coordinates are exact:** whole grid steps, or a fraction of them in lowest terms over a positive denominator where a cut crosses a sloped edge. No float is stored; SVG export writes millimetres exactly where the decimal ends and to the nanometre otherwise, the one rounding of an export (chapter 18.3).
- **Each line carries its source:** the element, the face or edge by its persistent name and role, cut or projected, and its category. Pens, colours and patterns are looked up from construct-styles' object styles at print time; the drawing holds no width (chapter 18.2: weights are paper sizes).
- **Faces are named through their planes for now.** `Engine::section` gives each cut edge the plane of the face it lies in (the face's carrier, chapter 20.4), its normal out of the solid and its coefficients with no common factor. The kernel builder gives the same plane for each named face of a recipe (`Kernel::solid`), so a cut edge finds its face's persistent name by plane. Faces on one plane, a wall's face split by an opening, share it, which is what a carrier reference wants. This stays until the seam carries the kernel's names.
- **The seam's cut:** `Engine::section(solid, plane)` gives closed loops, counter-clockwise round material seen from the plane's normal, each starting at its least point, in the order of those points. A plane that misses or only touches the solid gives no loops, as a value; every loop is checked closed.
- **Who answers it:** geom-solidcore's engine, exactly, from the kernel's own parts: `geom_bool::operand::prepare` for the solid's convex pieces, `geom_poly::cut::local_cuts` and `geom_poly::merge::section` for the meeting curves, and its own joining of them into loops. solidcore's `plane_section` is not the answer: it cuts only solids solidcore made (the kernel's prism and booleans keep no solidcore body), its curves are doubles and name no face. It is the oracle in a test. Unsupported for now, as values: curved faces, planes not square to a grid axis, and a corner of the solid on the plane, where chapter 18.4's tie rules wait for G389.
- **The stored form** is canonical JSON, one form only (`Drawing::to_bytes`, `from_bytes`), so the same model and view give the same bytes.

## Why

- Written to chapter 18's shapes (a view, cut curves keyed by element and source, cut regions keyed by element and material), so the kernel's engine fills the same values and the tests do not change (A12, decision 2).
- Grid-axis views are where every tolerance-based code fails (A12, "Plans and elevations on grid axes are the degenerate case"); with whole unit vectors nothing is rounded.
- Naming by plane needs nothing the seam does not have today, is exact, and matches the carrier references hosted elements already use (construct-model's `Want::Carrier`).
- The kernel already had exact section code for flat-faced pieces; using it follows the owner's rule to use the parts of our own kernel that exist.

## Step 4: regions merged by material, and hatched

The question: a plan's cut gives each element its own region and lines, so two walls of one material meeting at a corner draw a line between them, and no region is filled. Drawing practice wants one region, filled with the material's pattern, with a line only where materials change. Three things were open: which regions merge, what does the exact 2D work when the kernel does not answer it yet, and where the fill patterns live.

- **The rule.** Two cut regions merge when they have the same category and the same material and touch or overlap; a region with no material merges with nothing. Regions that touch only at a point stay apart; a hole stays a hole unless a region under the same rule fills it. Touching stands in for joined until joins exist (A12 step 7, job 9.2), when the rule becomes "joined, and the same category and material". Each edge of a merged outline keeps the source of the cut line it is part of (element, face name and role); a shortened edge keeps its source, and where the faces of two elements lie on one line the first in the drawing's order names it.
- **Derived, not stored.** Merged regions (`merge::merge`) and hatch lines (`hatch::hatch`) are made when a drawing is printed (`picture::picture`); the stored drawing keeps each element's own region and lines, so its form and its pinned bytes do not change (chapter 14.6: hatch lines are derived, never stored as truth).
- **The exact 2D work is ours, interim, in one module.** The kernel's 2D arrangement, booleans and hatches (`geom-2d`) are a scaffold and engine-geometry has no 2D operation, so there is nothing of the kernel's to call. A library is not used: `i_overlay` (MIT OR Apache-2.0), which A12 named, rounds the points where edges cross and works in 32-bit integers, and chapters 14.5 and 29 keep it for screen fills only, never for stored regions; here exactness is the point. So the crate has its own exact overlay, crate-private, behind one type (`overlay::Overlay`): every edge split at every crossing and touch; the winding number on each side of each piece counted from its midpoint moved by a symbolic step (each sign that would be zero is decided by the step's direction, so no tolerance anywhere); loops traced by the tightest left turn, which keeps point contacts apart; each hole given to the smallest outline holding it. Numbers are fractions of any size (num-rational's `BigRational` over num-bigint, MIT OR Apache-2.0, pinned, already in the lock and recorded in engine-geometry's licence table): where edges cross, a crossing's fraction can pass 128 bits, and is never rounded. It compares every edge with every other, enough for one material's walls in a plan; the interim register (`geom-solidcore/tests/interim.json`, programs) holds its row.
- **Hatch.** A fill pattern is families of parallel lines, each a whole direction and a printed spacing in micrometres measured along the sheet's x axis (y for a family along x), so every line is exact; line `k` passes through `(k s, 0)` or `(0, k s)` with `s` the spacing times the scale, every family from the view's origin so neighbouring regions' hatches line up. A line's pieces are those inside the region just to both sides of it, so a line along an edge or through a corner draws nothing, and a hole is never hatched. Lines are counted before any is made; more than 100,000 for one family is refused with the count, never cut short (14.6, "no silent caps").
- **Where the patterns live.** construct-materials gives each material a cut fill by name and no pattern table. Until construct-styles holds fill patterns as project data beside its line patterns, the table is `hatch::FillPatterns` in this crate, with one default pattern for each name the default materials use. The patterns are our own: thin continuous lines at 45 degrees as the general hatching of a cut (ISO 128-50's convention), told apart by spacing and a second family. They print with model pen 1 in the material's cut colour.
- **Weights.** A merged outline prints at its category's cut pen for the view's scale (construct-styles' object styles and pens table); where outlines of two regions lie on one another the line is drawn once, at the heavier pen, then by the drawing's order.
- **Outputs.** `picture::Picture` is the drawing ready to print at one scale, for any output; `picture::Output` writes it, and `svg::Svg` is the one writer today. A PDF writer is one more type.

## In the page: the server draws, the live tools stay (piece A65)

- **Who draws.** The server: `GET .../views/<view>/plan` (server `plan` module) reads the view, its range's cut height and the elements the view cuts, fills each recipe as the regenerator does, and calls construct-drawing through the kernel builder; the page draws the SVG that comes back and links no engine. Only a server built with the `kernel` feature has an engine that answers the cut, so any other refuses the route in words and the page says so in the plan pane. One element at a time: an element whose cut fails is named in `skipped`, never blanking the plan.
- **Layers.** `Kernel::layers` makes one solid per layer of a layered wall (its material, faces named as the wall names them), so a wall's layers hatch in their own materials. The wall's openings are not cut in them yet: a door shows as its own box over the whole wall until hosted families (job 6.4) cut the wall. Floors, grids and edges below the cut are not in the plan yet either.
- **Sharing the pane (the smallest step that keeps both working).** The server's picture is a group under everything live; the live elements stay in the SVG with their picking, claims, halos and tools, but a wall draws no fill or outline of its own while the picture is there (transparent, so a click still lands on it; a selected wall keeps its highlight). The picture's y runs up the sheet, the pane's down the model, so the group is flipped. The picture is asked for again 150 ms after any edit that changes the model or the view, as of the log's head, so a wall another person draws appears in it. A later piece may take the picking onto the picture's own element names (each line carries its element and face) and drop the live outlines.
- **The browser's list.** Model views sit beside the two fixed level plans the page always had (a model with no view record still opens a plan); opening one shows its level.

## Sections and elevations (piece A85)

The question: a section cuts the building with a vertical plane and shows what lies beyond it, to a depth; an elevation shows the building from outside with no cut. Both were stored already (construct-views' `CutLine`: two ends in plan, the side looked to, the depth), and the plan's cut worked along any grid axis. Four things were open: which lines can be drawn exactly, how the drawing gets what lies beyond the plane when the engine seam has no projection, what hides what before hidden-line removal exists, and how levels and grids are shown.

- **Lines along a grid axis only, as the plan's views.** `section::SectionView` takes a line from one end to the other, the side it looks to and its depth; the line must run along x or y, so the view looks north, south, east or west and every point maps exactly. A line at another angle is stored and refused when drawn, in words (`DrawingError::View`): the seam cuts only planes square to a grid axis (geom-solidcore's `section`), and the exact view predicates and sections of every body (G388, G389) lift it. A section cuts what its plane crosses, as the plan's cut does (`plan::plan` with the vertical plane); an elevation does not, and draws what lies beyond its plane only. The view's own frame (the distance along the line from its first end, and the height) maps to the drawing by `SectionView::point`: looking left, the distance runs to the right on the sheet, looking right to the left, so a crop held in that frame stays over the same part of the model when the line is flipped.
- **What is seen beyond is each solid's outline, made from exact cuts.** The seam's operations are extrude, subtract, loft, unite, volume, mesh (floating, drawn and never measured) and section; there is no projection. So the shadow of each solid between the plane and the depth is made from sections (the private `sweep` module): a flat-faced solid's cut square to the view changes its shape only at the depth of a corner, and every corner lies where three face planes meet, so the solid is cut once between each two such depths (never at one), neighbouring cuts of one shape are one stretch, and a stretch's shadow is its cut at the nearer depth and the piece of each face turned toward the viewer between its two depths, each corner where its faces meet the depth plane, exactly. The crate's overlay unites them; the boundary is the outline, one line per straight run, each named by the face it lies in or the edge where two meet (faces before edges, then by name, so a face seen edge on names its line). Every corner the engine gives is checked against its faces, or the answer is a refusal.
- **What hides what, meanwhile.** An outline piece is hidden where it lies inside or along a cut region (the plane is in front of everything beyond it) or inside or along the shadow of an element wholly nearer (its farthest depth at or before this one's nearest); both rules are exact and are what hidden-line removal will also decide there. Where two elements' depths overlap neither hides the other, and only outlines are drawn, not the edges inside them. The plan's own order (job 8.1 is cut regions and projected edges by view depth; 8.2 the visible and hidden edges of one element; 9.1 hidden edges between elements) does not let a full set of projected edges be drawn without the hidden lines taken out, since an edge a wall hides would show; so this piece draws the cut regions and the outlines of what is beyond, and says so: the inner edges are job 8.2's, the hiding between overlapping elements job 9.1's.
- **Levels and grids.** A level is a line across the view's width at its height, its name past the right end; a straight grid that meets the part of the plan the view sees and is not parallel to its plane is an upright line where its line, run on, crosses the plane, with a bubble on its top and its name in it; an arc grid is named as not shown (its crossing has a square root in it). Marks are drawn over the picture once it is cropped, inside the frame (the view's width and the heights the drawing and the levels reach, or the crop's box when it is on), never clipped. The sizes are our own (a 4 mm bubble, a name 2 mm past its line) and the lettering height is 2.5 mm, one of ISO 3098-0's heights for technical drawings. Names are written as SVG text after the drawing (`svg::Svg::labelled`); they are not stored in a drawing, as hatches and crops are not.
- **Through the log and the server.** A section is made by its line (`view.create`), moved by a vector (`view.line.move`), turned to look the other way (`view.line.flip`), its depth set (`view.set`); an elevation is made by the face it shows (`view.elevation.create`: the north elevation shows the north face, looking south, its line from its left end as one faces the building). Both line commands read the line as it is when they run, so moves add up in the log's order. The server draws a section or an elevation at `.../views/<view>/section` as it draws a plan, one element at a time, an element it cannot draw named with the reason.

Not yet, for the jobs that follow: the edges inside an outline (8.2), hidden edges between elements (9.1), seams between joined layers (9.2), section heads and elevation marks in other views (8.4), a datum's own extent per view, lines at other angles, and drawing solids with a wall's openings cut and for floors, roofs and placed families (the kernel builder's `Kernel::layers` gives walls whole and the boxes only, so the server names the others in `skipped`). The page that makes and opens a section is the piece's second half (below).

## Sections and elevations in the page (piece A85, second half)

- **Three page modules, no edit inside another tool.** `app/tools/views/sectionview.js` holds what the two tools share: the marks of the model's sections and elevations in the plan, the view opened in the plan pane, and the properties of a selected one. `section.js` is the section tool (two picks, the side it looks to and its far clip typed in its palette); `elevation.js` is the elevation tool (one press makes the building's four elevations, or one face chosen in its palette, through `view.elevation.create`). Each registers itself by the tool contract (note A96); `tools/index.js` has one line each. The View tab's Section and Elevation buttons are these tools.
- **What the page reads.** The views list (`/views`) gives a section's or an elevation's `line`: its two ends, `look` (`left` or `right`, walking from its first end) and `depth`, as the server writes lengths. The marks and the palette are drawn from it, so they follow the log: a change by anyone reads the list again.
- **The mark.** A line, a tail (a bar across it) at its first end and a head (a bubble with an arrow to the side it looks to, and its name) beyond its second. A press on a mark selects it and shows its properties (name, far clip, both ends, flip, open, crop and range, delete); a second press on it within 700 ms opens the view. Its far clip is the view's depth: how far it sees, square to its line.
- **Opening.** The view takes the plan's place in the plan pane (a layer that `replaces` the plan: `register.layers`, `replaces()`, the core's one new field; the live elements are not drawn while it does) and shows the server's drawing at `/views/<view>/section`: cut regions filled by material, outlines beyond, levels and grids, in the view's own x along its line and y up the sheet (so, unlike a plan's picture, it is not flipped). It is asked for again 150 ms after any change to the model or the view. A server with no geometry, or a line not along a grid axis, answers in words and the pane shows them.
- **Openings in the cut.** The server hands the builder each layered wall's doors and placed families (`ToDraw::opened`, seated as the regenerator seats them) and `Kernel::layers_hosting` cuts their openings through every layer they reach, so a section through a wall with a door shows the cut wall with the opening in it. A wall at an angle is drawn whole (its openings are united cells in the build, which a layer's solid does not take); a plan still draws its walls whole.
- **Not yet.** Lines not along a grid axis (the engine seam; G388, G389), the openings of an angled wall, dragging a section's line in the plan (its ends are typed in the properties; `view.line.move` moves it by a vector), far clip as a separate clip plane from the depth (A8-F1-P6).
