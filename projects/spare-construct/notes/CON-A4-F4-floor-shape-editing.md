# CON-A4-F4: A floor's top face shape-edited, and a type's variable layer

**Status, 2026-11-28:** Decided for the build (piece A99, job 14.15). construct-slabs' `top` holds a floor's points and split lines and works out the triangles they make, exactly; `Layers::with_variable` marks a floor type's variable layer; the stored forms are in its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-slabs/FORMAT.md). construct-regen's `floor_plan` reads the filled recipe for both builders and its `kernel` child `floor::edited` builds it through the engine seam. The commands are construct-api's `floor.shape.*` (`cloud/floors/shape.rs`), the page's Shape part of a selected floor, and a section of the two-browser test.

## The question

A sloped floor (A72, [the slope note](CON-A4-F4-P1-floor-slope-and-edges.md)) falls by one plane. A real floor falls to a waste from every side, folds along a valley or a ridge, sits on a wall or a beam that holds its top up, and keeps its structure one thickness while a screed takes up the falls. The owner asked for it in so many words (28 Nov: "modifying sub elements of floors"). Job 14.15 had to settle how such a top is held exactly on the length grid, how the layers follow it, what it reads back, and what is refused.

## How shape editing works in building modelling

A person adds points on the floor's top and gives each a height from the top (a waste 20 mm down), draws split lines between points where the top must fold, picks a supporting member whose line the top follows, and can reset the whole. The top becomes flat triangles between the points and the floor's corners, folding along the split lines; a modelling program draws those folds in plan. Every layer keeps its thickness and the floor bends, unless the type marks one layer variable: then the structure stays flat and that layer is thicker or thinner. A point lowered past what the variable layer allows is an error. (Checked against a reference modelling tool on a new blank project, 28 Nov: a 4 m by 3 m floor, 50 mm variable screed over 150 mm concrete, a point at its middle 20 mm down gives a flat bottom 200 mm down, 0.52 m3 of screed and 1.8 m3 of concrete, five vertices and eight creases; without the variable layer the bottom is 220 mm down under the point; 60 mm down is refused as too thin. Our numbers are the same.)

## The decisions

- **Points and split lines are the edits.** A point is an ID, a place in plan and a height from the floor's tied height (its level's elevation plus its offset). A point on a corner sets that corner's height; one on an edge divides the edge there (the side face stays upright and flat, its top edge bent); one inside the floor is a vertex of its own. A split line joins two points by their IDs; a line's height is its ends' heights. Every corner without a point stays at the top's height. The commands make the points a split line needs, each at the top's height where it is put, so drawing a line changes nothing until a height is set.
- **The top is the constrained Delaunay triangulation** of the corners and points, keeping every edge of the boundary and every split line as an edge, and otherwise as near equal-sided as they allow. Each triangle is flat through its three corners, which are grid points at whole grid steps of height, so every face is an exact plane and nothing is rounded. Four points on one circle (a plain rectangle's corners) may be divided either way: the order of insertion, fixed by the stored order, settles it; a person who wants the other fold draws a split line.
- **Our own exact triangulation, interim.** The kernel has an exact constrained Delaunay triangulation (geom-mesh's `triangulate`, piece G153), but it is internal to the kernel and the facade programs may use (engine-geometry) does not offer it, and editing the kernel's facade is not a Construct job. construct-slabs' `triangles` does the same with exact predicates (orientation in 128 bits, the circle test in num-bigint's integers), behind `Top`, so swapping it changes one file; the interim register has its row.
- **Plumb, as a sloped floor's.** A following layer hangs straight down from each triangle by its thickness: its sides upright, its volume the plan area times its thickness exactly. The reference tool measures a following layer's thickness square to each face (2.40017 m3 for the 2.4 m3 floor above); square thicknesses put corners at roots, so we keep A72's plumb rule.
- **The variable layer** is at most one layer of the type, never a membrane. Layers above it follow the top, layers below it stay flat where a plain floor has them, and it fills between: its volume is its plan area times its thickness plus the integral of the top's height over the floor (a sixth of a whole number of cubic steps, rounded once to the nearest cubic step). A point that would leave it no thickness is refused, naming the point. Without one every layer follows: the structure stays one thickness by default.
- **Stored as new versions, old bytes unchanged.** A shape-edited floor is recipe version 3 with its `top` (points and split lines) beside the boundary; a reset gives version 1 back byte for byte. A type with a variable layer is version 2 of the type's text, that layer marked; a type without one is version 1 as before. The filled recipe carries the triangles (each sharing an edge with one before it) and marks the variable layer only for a shape-edited floor, so plain floors fill the same whatever their type says.
- **Built as triangles lofted and united.** Each following layer, and the variable one, is each triangle's two flat sections lofted (every wall upright and flat) and united in order, each piece sharing a whole upright side with one before it, as a solved roof's pieces are; a layer below the variable one is the plain floor's extrusion. Holes are where no triangle is.
- **One or the other.** A floor that slopes by A72's arrow and has shape edits is refused in words, either way round (an arrow is one plane, edits are many); a floor with slab edges is not shape-edited yet; an opening or a shaft that would cut a shape-edited floor is refused by name (`ShapedFloorCut`) as through a sloped one.
- **Refused in words:** two points or lines with one ID, two points at one place, a point off the floor or in a hole, a point more than 10 m from the top, a split line naming no point, of no length or given twice, a split line leaving the floor, running along its edge, or crossing another (add a point where they cross), more than 500 points or lines.
- **Supports:** a wall is picked as a support, and the top gets a split line along its location line where it crosses the floor, its ends at the top's height there. Beams are not in the model yet, so only walls are offered.
- **What it reads back:** a shape-edited floor's highest and lowest points, and its layers' volumes and masses with the variable one's grown or shrunk. Its top area is not listed yet (a sum of roots per triangle; summed exactly it needs a bound worked out first). construct-slabs gives `Top::height_at` (the exact height at a plan point, a ratio of whole numbers), `Face::gradient` (each face's fall) and `Top::sixfold_rise` for whatever reads the top.

## Not here

Room volume less floors (job 6.3, piece A92) and spot slopes (piece A86) are not on main as this is written; when they land, a room's volume reads a shape-edited floor through `Top::faces` (each face a plane over a triangle, its underside the face less the thickness, or flat with a variable layer) and a spot slope through `Top::height_at` and `Face::gradient`, one call each. A flat roof has its own builder (construct-roofs), so it is not shape-edited by this job. Cutting a shape-edited floor with openings, slab edges under one, split lines that cross (cut where they cross, the crossing rounded once to the grid), the true top area, and beams as supports wait for their own jobs.
