# CON-A4-F3-P1: doors, windows and openings in walls at any angle

Part CON-A4-F3-P1 (hosted elements) of [FEATURES.md](../FEATURES.md), with CON-A7-F3-P2 (hosted families). Piece A83. Status: built in `construct-families` (`src/evaluate/seat.rs`, `Seat`; `evaluate_seated` and the upright boxes in `src/evaluate/build.rs`), `construct-regen` (`src/hosting.rs`, `seat`; `src/kernel/layered.rs`, the cuts of a wall at an angle; `src/kernel/opening.rs`, `Long`; `src/kernel/seated.rs`, a seated box; `src/kernel/family.rs`; `src/stand_in.rs`; `src/voids.rs`) and `construct-api` (`src/cloud/hosting.rs`: a host's fit asks each element as it is seated). The stored form is in [construct-model's FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-model/FORMAT.md), "Hosted elements" and "Placed families". It follows [note CON-A4-F3-P1-hosting](CON-A4-F3-P1-hosting.md) and main's ruling for the modify tools (piece A80, its note CON-A3-F4-P1-modify-tools): any angle, each point worked out exactly and rounded once to the grid.

## The question

An architect draws walls at any angle and puts doors and windows in them. The builder hosted a box door, a plain opening and a placed family only on a wall whose line runs along x or y: a door was a box along an axis, a family turned only by quarter turns, and every rule for an opening was asked along a plan axis. A wall at another angle has a line whose direction is a whole vector `d` of grid steps, but whose length `|d|` is in general irrational, and a point at a distance along it and across it falls between grid points. Where does a hosted element then sit, how are it and the opening its wall cuts kept together, which rules still hold and how are they asked, and what of a family's solids, which the kernel's interface cannot turn once they are made?

## Decisions

1. **The wall's own frame, exact.** Everything hosted on a layered wall at an angle is placed in the wall's frame: `a` along its line from its start, `s` square to it to its left, and up from its base. Each is a whole number of grid steps worked out exactly from the record's offsets and the wall's layer faces (the layer faces' offsets are the true distances from the line): a box door from the face of its side across by its own thickness toward the other side; a plain opening through the wall's whole thickness; a placed family's origin on the centre plane (to a whole step, as along an axis). Nothing is rounded there.
2. **One rounding, into the plan.** A frame point lands at `start + (a * d + s * d') / |d|`, `d'` being `d` turned a quarter to the left, each coordinate the nearest grid step, a half away from zero: the same one rounding the wall's own layer faces take (`wall_plan::Plan::across`), so a face corner of the wall is exactly the frame point on that face. It is done once, from whole numbers, never from another rounded point: `nearest(n / |d|)` is `(isqrt(4 n^2 / |d|^2) + 1) / 2`, signed as `n`, on num-bigint (MIT OR Apache-2.0; the products pass what an `i128` holds). The bound: each coordinate within half a step (78.125 nm, a hundredth of the model's tolerance of 0.01 mm) of the true point, on each axis. The tests check it on whole numbers, by squaring (construct-families' `tests/seated.rs`, construct-regen's `kernel_suite::lands_at`), at 30, 45 and 60 degrees and at a 3:4:5 slope, where every point on a multiple of five steps lands exactly.
3. **The seat: one frame for an element and its hole.** `hosting::seat` hands the builder each element hosted on a wall at an angle with its seat (`seat`, derived and never stored, like a family's `turn`): the way its own x runs (the wall's direction, or its opposite), the wall's start from the element's placement and the element's origin from there along that way and to its left (construct-families' `Seat`). Every point of the element's own shape (a box door's four corners, each of a family's extrusions) and every corner of the opening its wall cuts for it are worked out from that one frame, each rounded once. So the door and its hole share their jambs' along values exactly and each corner of both lies within half a step of its true place: the door stands in its hole as the grid allows, never a millimetre apart as two placements rounded to whole millimetres could leave it. Its placement is the nearest whole millimetre to its origin, a reference for its record, its bounds and its index cells only; the seat holds the rest. A seat a stored record holds is never handed on.
4. **The rules, in the wall's frame.** Every rule for an opening (past or at an end, below the base, too tall, overlapping, near a face, not in the wall) is asked of the frame values, as along an axis and in the same words. The wall's length is the root of its line's whole square length, `Long::Root`; a rule that sets an opening against the wall's end compares by squaring, so it is exact: a door ending exactly at a 3:4:5 wall's end touches it (`OpeningAtEnd`), and one past a wall's end at any angle crosses it (`OpeningPastEnd`), however little. A seated element runs along its wall by its seat, whatever axis its record names, so `OpeningAcrossWall` is not asked. An element handed to a wall at an angle without its seat (an older door hosted by its position, decision 4 of the hosting note) is `NotSeated`, naming `hosted.move`, which gives it its offsets; one seated for another wall (its way not this wall's line or its opposite, or its base and placement not this wall's start) is `SeatMismatch`.
5. **A family is seated, never mirrored.** Its `+y` points out of its side and its `+x` a quarter turn clockwise from that, as along an axis: its way is the wall's direction when it faces the line's left, and the opposite when it faces its right. Its solids are evaluated seated (`evaluate_seated`) and kept by its seat (`CacheKey::seated`): its corners round by where it stands, so the same type in two places is built twice.
6. **A family's upright profiles are upright boxes.** A profile on a level (`xy`) plane stays on one level plane however its corners round, and is extruded as it is. A profile on an upright plane (`xz`, `yz`: a door's frame and leaf, a window's frame, sash and glass) cannot: off the quarter turns an upright plane holds grid points only `|d| / gcd` apart along it, so three rounded corners of one upright face are in general not on one plane, and the engine takes no such profile (solidcore's `extrude`: "an outer corner off the polygon's plane"). A level rectangle extruded straight up stays a flat-faced prism whatever its corners round to, so such a profile is built as upright boxes: the box around its outline through its depth, less a cutter for each rectangle of what the outline leaves out of that box (a door frame's notch, reaching 10 mm under its foot) and for each rectangle of each hole (a window frame's light), each reaching 10 mm past both faces, so no face of a cutter lies on a face of the box. The rectangles are the region's slabs between corner heights, each stretch made as tall as the region lets it be. That holds for every profile whose edges run along its sketch's axes, as every template in the library does; one with a sloped edge is refused at an angle off the quarter turns (`UprightNotSquare`), interim (below).
7. **Walls along x or y, byte for byte.** A wall whose line runs along an axis hosts exactly as before: no seat is written, the door's box, the family's quarter turn and every rule are as they were. Pinned: construct-regen's `walls_along_the_axes_host_byte_for_byte_as_before` builds a box door, a wall opening, a placed door and a placed window on either side of a partition on each of the four axis ways, on the kernel's builder and the stand-in, and its digest was taken on main before this piece changed anything.
8. **The stand-in draws what is seated turned with its wall.** A seated box door is the prism on its plan rectangle laid along its own x, and each of a seated family's solids the prism on its box's plan rectangle in the family's own frame, every corner put through the seat and then the nearest whole millimetre; a seated wall opening is drawn from its seat's origin along its way. It still cuts nothing.
9. **One fit, as built.** A host's fit (construct-api's `hosting::fit`) asks the kernel builder's rules of each element as its host's builder is handed it, through `hosting::seat` (the same hunk as piece A80's fix for a family on a wall along y), so what a command allows and what is built cannot differ at any angle.

## Interim

- A family's profile on an upright plane with a sloped edge (an arched or angled head) is refused seated off the quarter turns (`UprightNotSquare`): the kernel's `Engine` interface cannot turn a solid once it is made, and only upright boxes keep their faces flat at such an angle. Replaced when the kernel's engine can turn or place a solid (a later kernel piece with the exact kernel): every profile is then built turned as it is, and decision 6 goes. The interim register's row for the kernel's family solids says so.
- The stand-in cuts no opening, as before.

## Not done here, in order

1. The page: connect.js seats a hosted element in its JS (`onLayered`, `onFamily`, `seatOfPiece`) for walls along an axis only; it must hand the browser's builder the same seat (or ask the package for it), and the package (app/pkg) be rebuilt, or a wall at an angle with a door in it builds differently in the browser and its checkpoint does not match. A two-browser section placing a door in a wall at an angle.
2. A plan view draws a hosted element from its record and its stored recipe (the server's `plan.rs`), not seated: a door in a wall at an angle is drawn along its own axis there, and a placed family not at all, as along an axis today. The views' job: draw a hosted element seated.
3. Moving a wall from an axis to an angle (piece A80's rotate) refused while it hosts something; once both are on main, that refusal goes and nothing in the modify tools changes.

## Risks

- A seated family is built once per place rather than once per type, since its corners round by where it stands: a wall at an angle with many copies of one window builds each.
- An upright profile with many notches makes many cutters, each one engine subtraction; the library's templates make at most one per solid.
