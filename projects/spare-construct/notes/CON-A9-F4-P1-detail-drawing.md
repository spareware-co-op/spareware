# CON-A9-F4-P1 and P2, CON-A9-F1-P2: detail drawing in a view

Parts CON-A9-F4-P1 (detail tools), CON-A9-F4-P2 (detail components) and CON-A9-F1-P2 (spot slopes) of [FEATURES.md](../FEATURES.md); piece A86 (job 14.8, the data and commands half). Status: built in `construct-detailing` (the data, the checks, the placing, the slope and the drawing of a view's items) and `construct-api` (`src/cloud/detailing.rs`: 22 `detail.*` commands and the read-only listing); the server's plan drawing lays a view's items over its model. The stored form is [construct-detailing's FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-detailing/FORMAT.md). The page's tools, a two-browser section and the package rebuild are the second half.

## The question

A real architect's model (the sample the plan's wave 14 was written from) holds 1,184 detail lines, 231 detail items, 38 filled regions and 194 spot slopes beside its building. None of them is part of the building: each is drawn in one view, over the model, and shown nowhere else. How are they held so that they are in the one ordered edit log like everything else, never reach a schedule or another view, keep every length exact on the grid, follow the model where they read it, and can be drawn by the same pens and patterns as the rest of the drawing?

## Decisions

1. **An item is an element record with no shape, naming its one view.** Seven kinds begin `detail.` (the table in FORMAT.md). Each is a record of the document, so the edit log, undo, redo, claims, replay and packages treat it as they treat a grid or a view; none is built, because the regenerator leaves a record whose kind begins `detail.` without a shape. The prefix is tested there (`has_shape`) and not by naming construct-detailing, because that crate depends on construct-modify (for the turn), which depends on construct-regen: a cycle. A test in construct-api (`every_detail_kind_is_one_the_regenerator_leaves_without_a_shape`) holds the two to one list. Each kind has its category (detail lines, filled regions, masking regions, detail components, spot dimensions), so a view's graphics rules can hide it as they hide any category.
2. **Lines and arcs; a line style is data in the project.** A detail line is straight or an arc. The arc is the sketch solver's (centre, start, end, anticlockwise), and the solver's `check_drawn` is the check: the end lies on the circle within 0.01 mm. A line's style is a model pen of construct-styles (so it prints thinner at a small scale, as every model line does), a line pattern and a colour. Six are built in under our own names (`fine`, `standard`, `bold`, `centre`, `hidden`, `dotted`); a person's own are `detail.linestyle` records, each with its own ID and a name unique among them (case aside, and never a built-in's). An item names its style and never holds a copy, so one change to a style changes every line, and a style in use cannot be deleted. The pens and patterns are the default metric set, as the plan's drawing uses them, until construct-styles' tables are project data.
3. **A region's outline is an exactly simple loop, checked as a view's crop outline is.** At least three corners, none twice in a row, no two edges that cross, touch or overlap, an area that is not zero, in whole numbers on the grid, no float and no tolerance. The predicates are the crop's (piece A79, not on main yet) written again in this crate's own types, because they must be exact on 128-bit whole numbers, which no floating-point geometry library gives. A filled region has a fill pattern (by name) and a colour for its lines, and a boundary line style or none; a masking region is opaque paper with no pattern and is a record of its own kind, so one cannot become the other. The area is exact, twice the area being a whole number of square steps; its value in square steps is rounded half up and marked inexact only when twice the area is odd.
4. **A component is a recipe of its own kind, not a family recipe.** A detail component has lines and fills in its own frame and nothing else: no parameters, no 3D, no host, no type table. Widening the family recipe's schema for it would put a view-only thing inside the family editor's format and make every family reader carry it, so it is a small JSON document with its own schema (`detail-component-1`), reader and checker, in the library folder beside the door and window recipes. Lengths are typed text read by construct-params; the written form is the exact millimetres. Three designs of our own ship: a timber section, a blocking cross and an insulation batt. A placed component names its recipe by key, a position and an angle held modulo a full turn.
5. **Any angle, by the one rule.** A turn is construct-modify's `Turn` of the angle, and each point is its `Motion::apply` of the recipe's point about the position: exact for quarter turns, and for any other angle within half a step of the true place on each axis, rounded once. A component is therefore never turned from an already rounded point.
6. **A repeat is a line and a spacing; the copies are worked out.** The first copy is at the start and one more every spacing along the line while it fits: along 3 m at 600 mm there are six. The count is `floor(length / spacing) + 1`, exact (the floor of a square root is the square root of the floor), and each copy's place is the nearest grid step to its exact place along the line, worked out in whole numbers of any size so that a long or slanted line never drifts (the 1000 mm copies along (1000, 1000) are 4,525,483 steps on each axis, the nearest to 6,400,000 / sqrt 2). At most 2,000 copies are made at once, so a mistyped spacing of 1 mm over 100 m is refused by name instead of making 100,001. Each copy lies along the line and is then turned by the repeat's extra angle. Neither the count nor the places are stored.
7. **A spot slope is a face and a point, and a value that is never stored.** The slope is read from the face at the log's head each time it is asked, as a dimension is. A floor's face is level, so it refuses by name (`flat`); a roof's faces are the planes of its top face (construct-roofs' `shape`), and the one over the point gives the plane through three of its corners exactly. The reading is the exact square of the rise over the run, the rise over the run itself when it is a fraction of whole numbers that fits 128 bits (a face rising 3 in 4 along one axis is 3/4; a roof pitched at 30 degrees, as built on the grid, is the fraction of its whole steps), the angle to the nearest angle unit (one float, from libm's `atan2`, within one unit, display only), and the way water runs. A refusal is by name: `missing`, `off-face`, `flat`, `vertical`. Creating or moving a slope that would have none is refused; a slope that loses its face later (the roof goes flat, or is deleted) stays and is a warning of the same name in the shape construct-warnings gives, never a wrong number. A spot slope's point is in plan, so it is made only in a plan.
8. **The server's drawing of a view includes them.** construct-drawing gains `Over`: solid fills, pattern lines and lines laid on a `Picture`, written by the SVG after everything else (fills, then pattern lines, then lines), so a masking region hides the model and a detail line shows over it. The server's plan passes the view's prepared items to the drawer, which renders them with the default pens and fill patterns and leaves out any category the view hides. An item that cannot be drawn is named in the plan's `skipped` with the reason, so one item never blanks the plan. A spot slope is drawn as an arrow 8 mm of paper long down the fall; its numbers wait for the text engine (job 7.4) and are in the listing.

## What is read-only

`GET /api/projects/{project}/models/{model}/views/{view}/detail` lists a view's items and the six built-in styles and the document's own: each line's points, each region's corners and exact area, each component's recipe and position, each repeat's count and places, each slope's reading or its warning by name, and the library's components. Nothing in it is stored.

## Not done here, in order

1. The page's tools (draw a detail line, a region, place and repeat a component, pick a face for a spot slope, a line style editor), the two-browser section and the package rebuild (the second half).
2. Text beside a spot slope, a detail component's own label, and line patterns drawn dashed (construct-drawing's SVG prints every pattern solid); all wait for the text engine and the pattern writer.
3. Mirroring a placed component, parameters in a component recipe, and detail items in a legend or a drafting view's own sheet frame; and modify tools (move, copy, rotate, mirror, array) for detail items, which `modify.*` refuses by name today.
4. Clipping the over-layer to a view's crop (piece A79's `crop::clip` knows the picture's strokes and hatches, not `Over`): when the crop lands on main it clips `Over` the same way.
5. Section and elevation drawing, which the plan drawing does not do yet (job 8.1): items in those views are stored and listed, and drawn when the views are.

## Risks

- A document with hundreds of items in a view reads every record of the document on each listing and each plan, as every other listing does today; the records are small and the read is one scan.
- The roof's planes are read through construct-roofs' `shape` with the roof type's layers, which is the cost of one roof build; a view with many spot slopes on one roof reads it once per slope's face, once per face.
