# CON-A4-F6-P1: Curtain walls, grids, panels and mullions (first half)

**Status, 2026-11-28:** Decided for the build (piece A84, the data and shape half of job 14.1). The crate `construct-curtain` follows it and its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-curtain/FORMAT.md) is the stored form; construct-regen's `curtain_plan` and `curtains` read a curtain wall for both builders, and construct-api's `cloud::curtains` holds its commands. The page's curtain wall tool, its drawing in plan and 3D and its two-browser section are the second half.

## The question

A real architect's model holds many curtain walls (one sample building: 65 curtain walls with 235 grid lines, 681 panels and 1,425 mullions), and the plan had no job for them. Job 14.1 asks for the first part: a curtain wall on a straight line, a grid by spacing or count, panels (glazed, solid, a door), mullions by type, as a wall type like any other. This note settles what is a type's and what is a wall's, how a grid is laid out and edited, where each panel and mullion stands exactly, how they meet, how a curtain wall sits in a basic wall, and what it reads back.

## How curtain walls are built and drawn

A curtain wall is a light frame hung on the building: vertical and horizontal members (mullions; the horizontal ones are often called transoms) on a grid, with infill panels (glass, or an opaque spandrel panel) held between them. Its grid is set out from a module, as a regular spacing or a count of equal bays, and a designer moves, adds or removes lines where the plan needs them, joins bays into wider panels, and puts doors and vents into bays. At each crossing one member runs through and the other is cut to it: the vertical members usually run through (stick systems), sometimes the horizontal. A curtain wall placed in the run of a masonry or framed wall is set into an opening in it.

## The decisions

- **A curtain wall is its own kind** (`curtain`, recipe `wall.curtain`, the curtain walls category 200, whose subcategories Mullions and Panels already exist), on a straight line between levels: its base and top tied as a wall's, or a height. Curved curtain walls wait for curved walls (job 14.4).
- **The type says the grid and the parts; the wall keeps only its own edits.** A type holds, in its `@system.curtain` block: for each direction a layout (none; a fixed spacing justified at the start, the centre or the end; a fixed count of equal panels; a most spacing, the fewest equal panels none wider), the panel every cell holds, four mullion slots (interior vertical, interior horizontal, border vertical, border horizontal; each a profile or none) and which direction runs through at a crossing. A wall's recipe keeps its line, its height and, only when it has any, its edits: grid lines it adds, lines of its type it removes, segments it leaves out, panels it swaps. So changing the type changes every wall of it, and each wall's own changes stay.
- **Every edit is named by where it stands**, never by a count or an index: a vertical line by its distance along the wall from its start, a horizontal one by its height above the base, a segment by its line and the crossing it starts at, a panel by the corner its cell starts at. Lengths are construct-params' `Length` in grid steps, so a place is exact. An edit the grid no longer has a place for (the type changed under it) is kept and does nothing, and the layout lists it as unused: a type change never fails a wall, and changing it back brings the edit back.
- **Layout rules** (construct-curtain's `grid`): a centred spacing puts a line on the middle and so makes the two end panels equal; equal divisions are each the nearest grid step (a half up), the one rounding, so neighbouring panels differ by at most 156.25 nm; a line within the model's tolerance (0.01 mm) of a border is that border.
- **The borders stand inside the wall.** A border mullion's outer face is on the wall's start, end, base or top, so a curtain wall never reaches past its own line or height, and an embedded one's opening is exactly its extent. An interior mullion is centred on its line, so its width must halve on the grid (an odd number of steps is refused, never rounded).
- **Panels fill their cells to the mullions' faces** (each edge line's band), whether a mullion stands there or not, so a panel's size never depends on which mullions are left out. Cells either side of a left-out segment are one panel; every panel must be a rectangle of cells, and an edit that would make another shape is refused. A kept segment inside a merged panel carries no mullion: a mullion stands only between two panels or on a border.
- **Crossings:** where a vertical and a horizontal line cross, the type's direction runs through if it has a mullion there, otherwise the other does. One that runs through meets the next along its line at the crossing, or covers the whole crossing to the far side of the other line's band where none follows it; one that does not run through stops at the other's band. No two solids overlap and every meeting is face to face (a test checks every pair).
- **In plan**, a frame point `(u, w)` (along, across toward the exterior) is `start + A(u) + R(w)`: `A(u) = (end - start) * u / L`, with `L` the line's length to the nearest step so the far border lands exactly on the line's end, and `R(w)` the layered wall's own offset across. Each corner is rounded once along and once across; along an axis nothing is rounded. Every solid is a parallelogram on grid points extruded straight up, which the kernel's exact prism takes.
- **A door panel** is a door family of the library, sized to its panel (its family's `width` and `height` set to the panel's), standing on the panel's foot at its middle, centred on the line, turned by quarter turns so that its `+y` points out of the exterior. Only a curtain wall along x or y takes one, since a family turns by quarter turns only. On the kernel its solids are the recipe evaluator's through the engine; the seam has no move of a solid, so they are built at the family's origin and their meshes moved by whole grid steps (interim: the register's row).
- **Embedding.** A curtain wall placed in a layered wall is hosted on a side of it as a plain opening is (a face reference and its offsets along and up). Its own line is its host's reference line from that offset for its own length, so it moves with its wall; its host is handed the plain opening it cuts, its length by its height through the whole thickness, so every rule and refusal of a plain opening applies unchanged (inside the wall's ends, below its top). A curtain wall as high as its wall would cut it in two, which the engine's one-solid subtraction does not give: refused, until a wall is built from every piece a cut leaves.
- **Joins.** The main branch has no wall joins yet (L and T joins of layered walls come with job 5.1's train); a curtain wall's ends are square at its line's ends, and walls meet it as they meet each other today.
- **What it reads back** (read-only parameters, worked out each time, never stored): its length, height and area (length by height), how many panels and their area (each its own, between the mullions' faces), the glazed panels' area, and how many mullions and their length (each its run after the crossings). Worked by hand: 6 m by 3 m at 1.5 m by 1 m is 12 panels of 1.5 by 1 m (18 m2) with no mullions; with 50 mm mullions on every line and border, the vertical ones running through, 12 panels of 16.1 m2 in all and 31 mullions of 38 m in all. (The same wall at 1.5 m by 1.5 m is 8 panels.)
