# CON-A4-F3-P1: hosting, a door on a face of its wall

Part CON-A4-F3-P1 (hosted elements) of [FEATURES.md](../FEATURES.md). Piece A49. Status: built in `construct-model` (`Geometry::build_hosting`, the `reads` row), `construct-regen` (`src/hosting.rs`, the regenerator, the builders' face names and `kernel::fits`) and `construct-api` (`src/cloud/hosting.rs`: `hosted.place`, `hosted.move`, and the hosting rules of `element.*` and undo). The stored form is in [construct-model's FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-model/FORMAT.md), "Hosted elements".

## The question

A door's record must say what it is hosted on so that its wall is built with its opening, rebuilt when the door changes, and so that the door moves with its wall without its record being rewritten. How is that stored, how does a record written before this find its host, where does an offset put a door, and what happens when the wall no longer has room for it?

## Decisions

1. **The record.** A hosted element names its host by one reference of kind `face` to a side of the host, by the face's persistent name (chapter 20.4: by name, never by place in a list), `want` `carrier` so an opening through the face does not break it, the face's role kept as the `signature` for a repair only. Its offsets along the face and up it are two `props`, `@host.along` and `@host.up`, each a construct-params stored length. The `@` keys are the server's (no `element.*` command sets them), so only the hosting commands write them. Nothing else in the record changes shape, so every record written before reads as it did.
2. **Where an offset puts it.** Along the wall's axis from its start (`end0`), whichever side it is on, so lengthening a wall at its end leaves its doors where they are; up from its base; across, flush with the side it is hosted on, so a door as thick as its wall fills it. This is a fixed rule because a door is still a box: a family that declares its host and the void it cuts (CON-A7-F3-P2) replaces it. A placement is whole millimetres, so an offset finer than that is refused (`NotWholeMillimetres`), not rounded.
3. **The face is named without building.** A door names its wall's face before the wall is built again with it, so the names are the builders' own, written from the recipe ([`face_names`](../crates/construct-regen/src/lib.rs)): the stand-in's `box.<role>`, the kernel builder's `NameScheme v1` ids. They hold no length, so a wall rebuilt, lengthened or moved keeps them.
4. **How an old door finds its host.** Before this, the page wrote a door as a `door.single` placed by its position, with a reference of kind `face` to its wall's exterior and no offsets. Such a record is hosted by the element that reference names, and it sits where its own placement puts it: its place in its host is the difference of the two placements (the kernel builder's own `Units::offset` rule). So its wall is built with its opening, and the wall is not deleted from under it, but it stays where it was placed when the wall moves, as it always did. `hosted.move` writes its offsets, and from then on it moves with its wall. Anything else without offsets is not hosted. The rule is a reading of the record, not a search: no door is attached to a wall it does not name.
5. **Dependents, through the one `reads` table.** The regenerator's derived `reads` row of a hosted element names its host; its host's row lists what it hosts, in key order. A change to a door builds its wall again (the host it had and the host it has); a change to a wall builds each of its doors again, because a door's place, and so the cells it is indexed under, is read from its wall. Each such rebuild is one step deep, from the record each built row names, as a type's elements are rebuilt; so a rebuild from the log on an empty store gives the same bytes. A row that holds only a type, as before, keeps its old spelling.
6. **A wall shortened past its door: reported, by the builder's own rule.** The kernel builder refuses an opening that crosses or touches an end; it never moves or clips a door. So a wall made too short for its doors, by `element.set` or by a type's parameter, is built as failed, in the builder's words, naming the door (`"door D-1: it crosses the wall's end"`), as every build failure is model state. The person moves or deletes the door, or lengthens the wall again. Refusing the wall's edit instead would mean a command building or re-planning every wall a parameter feeds; the hosting commands, which place one door on one wall, do refuse at once.
7. **The hosting commands refuse in the builder's words, on every edition.** `hosted.place` and `hosted.move` ask the kernel builder's rules for openings without the engine ([`kernel::fits`](../crates/construct-regen/src/kernel.rs)), on the document's grid step and tolerances, or on the kernel's grid and the model's 0.01 mm for the stand-in, which has no engine. So a model drawn on the stand-in holds no door the kernel builder would refuse when it moves to the kernel.
8. **A host keeps what it hosts.** Deleting an element that still hosts others is refused, naming how many. An undo that would leave a hosted element without its host (an undo of the wall's creation after someone placed a door on it, or of a door's deletion after its wall went) is turned back whole.
9. **No new crate.** The plan proposed `crates/construct-hosting`. The reading of a hosted record is shared by the regenerator and the commands, so it sits in construct-regen beside the parameter reader that already is (`src/parameters.rs`), and the commands in their own file of construct-api.

## Interim

- The stand-in builds a wall alone, whatever is hosted on it (the interim register's row): no opening is cut until a document is on the kernel's edition.
- Only a `wall.straight` hosts, only on its two sides, and a hosted element sits flush with its side (decision 2).

## Not done here

- Windows, plain openings and shafts (CON-A4-F3-P2); a door's swing and its family (CON-A7-F3-P2).
- Hosting across a linked document (`doc` set on the reference).
