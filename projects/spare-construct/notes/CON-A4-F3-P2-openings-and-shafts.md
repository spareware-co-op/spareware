# CON-A4-F3-P2: Openings and shafts

**Status, 2026-11-23:** Decided for the build (piece A66, job 4.2, the shape half). The crate `construct-openings` follows it and its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-openings/FORMAT.md) is the stored form; construct-regen's `kernel` cuts them (its `opening` module for walls, its `floor` module for floors), its `voids` module draws them on both builders, and its `regenerator` hands each floor its openings and the shafts that pass through it. The commands, the page tools and the browser's package are a later piece.

## The question

An opening is a hole cut through a host that is not a door or a window: a rectangular hole through a wall, a hole of any outline through a floor, and a shaft, an outline cut through every floor between two levels. Before commands could make them, job 4.2 had to settle what each record holds, how it names its host or its reach so regeneration rebuilds the right things, which path cuts it, what is refused, and how its quantities are checked.

## The decisions

- **One cutter for walls.** A wall opening is cut on the door's own path (the kernel's `opening`): the same frame, the same refusals in the same words, the same tool names for its faces (`tool(<key>).jamb.start`, `.jamb.end`, `.head`, `.sill`). It differs from a door only in its span: its width and height are stored lengths, and it always goes through the wall's whole thickness. A refusal names it `opening <ID>`.
- **Hosted as a door is.** A wall opening's position along the wall and its sill height are the hosting record's `@host.along` and `@host.up`, on one face reference; it moves with its wall, and the regenerator's `reads` rows (`host`, `hosted`) rebuild the wall when either changes, with nothing new.
- **A floor opening is in its floor's plan.** Its outline is drawn in the floor's own frame and it names the floor's top face with no offsets, a third way of being hosted (`InPlan`): it sits where its floor does. The same `reads` rows link the two.
- **A floor's cut is the floor's own path.** The kernel builds a floor as each layer's profile extruded; its openings and shafts are more holes in that profile, through every layer, so there is no second cutter and no subtraction. The fit is the floor's own boundary checks (construct-slabs' `Boundary`) with the outlines as more holes: wholly inside, touching nothing of the edge, apart from each other and from the floor's own holes. Their sides are named as a tool's faces, `tool(<key>).side.<i>`.
- **A shaft's reach is its levels.** A shaft holds its base and top ties as a wall does, and its height is filled from them; its own `reads` row lists both levels, so a level's move builds it again. It is hosted on nothing: a floor reads every shaft of its document (their kind rows) each time it is built, and a shaft built again builds every floor of its document again, one step deep. So a floor made later inside its reach is cut, one that moves out of it is not, and nothing is stored that could go stale. A floor is cut when the shaft passes wholly through its thickness; touching only its top or bottom face, or lying beside it in plan, misses it.
- **Refused rather than built wrongly**, each by name and in words: a curved outline (the engine seam's extrusion takes polygons; held in the data, refused until it can), a shaft ending inside a floor's thickness (a part-depth cut is a recess, a later piece), a shaft taking a whole floor, a wall opening in a wall at an angle (as a door is, until hosted families). Each has its row in the interim register.
- **Quantities are exact.** construct-openings works out what each layer holds after the cut in construct-params' units (a wall layer's side less its openings, a floor layer's area less its holes and cut outlines), and the kernel builder's suite measures the solids the engine builds against the same numbers, exactly.
- **No new engine method.** Extrude with holes and subtract, both already on the seam, do it all (decision 32.70 stands).
- **Voids are drawn.** An opening and a shaft have no solid of their own; both builders draw them (`voids`): a wall opening's rectangle in its wall's plane, a floor opening's outline, a shaft's outline with a ribbon up to its top, the body being the recipe's own canonical bytes, as a datum's is.
