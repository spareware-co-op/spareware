# CON-A4-F7-P1: Rooms found from a closed loop of walls

**Status, 2026-11-23:** Decided for the build (piece A67, job 5.4, the shape half). The crate `construct-rooms` follows it and its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-rooms/FORMAT.md) is the stored form; construct-regen's `rooms` finds a room's loop from the walls of its level and says which rooms a change builds again, its `room_plan` reads the filled recipe for both builders, and its `kernel` module's child `room` builds the room's volume through the engine seam. The commands (`room.place`, `room.rename`, `room.number`, `room.move`, `room.upper`, `room.delete`, in construct-api's `cloud/rooms.rs`), the server's read-only view and the page's Room tool and tags are the second half (piece A67, second lane).

## The question

A room is the space a person names and numbers (Kitchen, 101): it is not drawn, it is found from the walls round it, and it must follow them. Before rooms could be placed from the page, job 5.4 had to settle what a room record holds, which face of a wall bounds it, how its loop is found and kept exact, what becomes of a room whose walls do not close, and how it is kept up to date.

## How rooms are found and measured

A room is placed by a point inside it on a level. Its boundary is where the walls round that point stand; a wall that stops short leaves the space open, and the room is reported as not enclosed until it is closed. A floor area is measured in plan inside the bounding walls; a volume up to the room's ceiling or the level above.

## The decisions

- **The record:** kind `room`, the rooms category (3300), its name in the record's `name` and its number as text under `@room.number`, its point in its recipe (`at`, two stored lengths), its base tied to its level, and its upper limit either the level above (its top tie, `height` written `{"tie":"top"}` and filled exactly by the regenerator) or a given height (a stored length). The recipe's `walls` argument, `{"room":"walls"}`, is filled with the room's loop before it is built, as a wall's layers are filled from its type.
- **Which face bounds a room:** the plan does not say, so the wall's **finish face toward the room** (whichever of its two faces that is). A room measured to the finished face is what a person paces out and what a room schedule lists first; measuring to the centre line or the core is an area scheme's rule (job A4-F7-P2, area plans), which reads the same loop and the walls' layers.
- **The search is our own exact plane geometry** (the crate's `plane` module, the header says why): the walls' outlines in plan, exactly as the builders lay them out on the grid, are taken together as solid; every edge is cut where another meets it, the pieces that are an edge of the walls together are kept, joined into loops turning to the first piece anticlockwise at each corner, and the smallest clockwise loop round the point is the room. Anticlockwise loops directly inside it are holes. Crossing points are exact rationals (dashu, as the formulas and the kernel's exact oracles use).
- **Walls that only touch at a corner close the loop** there: their faces meet and leave no gap a person could pass; a gap of one grid step opens it. A T-meeting makes two rooms whether the partition runs into the wall or stops at its face. A wall ending inside a room is walked round; an island wall or a column is a hole.
- **A door or a window does not break the loop:** a wall's outline is read whole, openings and all. When openings land (piece A66), they cut the wall's solid, not the outline the search reads.
- **Exact, or refused by name:** every corner of a room must be a grid point and its area a whole number of square steps. A corner where two slanted faces cross off the grid is refused (`OffGrid`, naming the walls), and an odd twice-area too (`AreaNotExact`); nothing is rounded. The perimeter is the one rounding, each edge to the nearest step, as a floor's; exact along an axis. A person reads every measure in their units, rounded only there.
- **Not enclosed and redundant are outcomes, not refusals:** the record stays and its built row says `NotEnclosed`, or `Redundant` naming the room whose ID comes first in the same loop. When that room goes, the other takes the loop over. The first by ID, not by time: the same on every machine, with no clock.
- **Kept up to date by the regenerator:** a room's `reads` row lists its levels and the walls its loop runs along. A wall that changes, is made or is deleted builds again every room on the levels it was and is on, and every room whose row lists it; a room that changes builds again the other rooms of its levels. One step deep, in key order, as hosting is. The walls are read as regeneration has reached them, so a room is the same however the log was reached.
- **Which walls are read:** every wall whose base is on the room's level; a layered wall by its outline as its joints with the walls it meets shape it (the whole wall's outline of the recipe it is built from, construct-walls' join rule, piece A60), or by the four corners of its outer faces where no joint changes it; the old box wall by its box. So where two walls join, the room's faces are the walls' faces as joined and meet where the joint puts them: at a corner that points into the room, at one point (issue 1329; read from each wall's own rectangle, that corner kept a notch of the half thickness squared). A wall that does not build (its type missing) is not there to bound a room, as it is in no view. Walls from another level that rise through this one are not read yet.
- **Its volume as a shape:** on the kernel's edition one solid, its loop with its holes extruded from its base to its upper limit through the engine seam, passed by the kernel's checker; faces `bottom`, `top` and `side.<k>`. The stand-in builds the box round its outer loop (its interim row). No engine method was added.
- **Interim, refused by name:** a curved wall on the room's level (`CurvedWall`; none can be drawn yet) until the kernel's exact arcs reach the walls, and a sloped upper limit (`SlopedLimit`) until rooms are bounded by floors, ceilings and roofs (job 6.3). Both are rows of the interim register.

## Why

- A room found from its walls, kept by the same `reads` rows as every derived shape, cannot fall out of step with them, and an AI or a script reads why a room has no area from its built row.
- Exact corners and areas keep every machine and every replay giving the same numbers, as the stored-units note requires ([CON-A2-F3-P1](CON-A2-F3-P1-stored-units.md)).
- The finish face is what a room is measured to before any scheme says otherwise.

## Not decided here

- Rooms bounded by ceilings and roofs (job 6.8 and the roof jobs): a room is bounded by its levels and floors only until they are built.
- National measurement rules for areas (A4-F7-P3).

## Room separation lines and room volume (piece A92, job 6.3)

**Status, 2026-11-28:** Decided for the build. The stored form is in the crate's [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-rooms/FORMAT.md) ("Room separation lines", "Room volume"); area plans are in [CON-A4-F7-P2](CON-A4-F7-P2-area-plans.md).

- **A line is a zero-thickness bound of the same search.** A room separation line is a segment in plan that bounds the open space on both of its sides (both directions are kept as pieces, as a wall's face is one). Inside a wall's outline or along a wall's face the wall stands and the line is dropped; two lines along each other, the earlier ID stands. A line with a loose end, and a line that ties one loop to another, is a spike or a bridge in a face's walk: it is cut out, so every loop is simple and a room's perimeter never counts a line twice. A line that crosses nothing or bounds nothing is kept: a person may draw it first and close the walls after.
- **Any angle, rounded once.** A line's corner where it meets a wall's face or another line is a rational point, on the grid only by luck. Refusing every angled line would make them useless, so the corner is rounded once, to the nearest grid step (a half up), the same for both rooms either side of it, and the loop's area is then rounded once, a half up, instead of refused as inexact. A corner between two walls only keeps the old rule, refused by name. This is main's rule for any angle (exact rationals, each point rounded once to the nearest grid step) kept with the rationals the search has always used (dashu), so no second big-number type enters the crate.
- **Moving or deleting a line finds the rooms it touches again** by regeneration alone: a line is a spatial kind, so every room on its level is built again, and a room whose loop runs along it lists it in its `reads` row.
- **Volume less the floors.** A room's height is from its base to its upper limit; what floors in the space take is the integral, over the floor's plan inside the room, of `max(0, min(top, upper) - max(top - thickness, base))`. The floor above takes its thickness from the room's top, a raised floor what stands above the base, a floor at the base nothing, a sloped floor by its plane. Exact (rationals, Green's theorem over the intersections of polygons with holes, strips between lines of equal top height for a slope), rounded once at the end. It is worked out in construct-api, which can read the document's floors and their openings; the filled recipe the builders take is unchanged. A floor's slab edges are not counted (they hang below its underside) and ceilings are not built, so rooms are bounded by levels and floors only until job 6.8, which adds its underside as one more surface in the same integral.
- **Lines are not schedule rows.** A room separation line carries the rooms category, so schedules skip it; the Rooms schedule's `Volume` field is the same net number.

## The commands and the page (second half)

- **One entry, one undo step each**, claims as the floor commands. A room's number is unique in its document: a number another room has is refused, naming that room. The next free number is one more than the largest number made only of digits (as wide as that one), or 1; the page is offered it from `/rooms` and may send another.
- **The search's refusals are the commands'**: every refusal of `found_at` (a point in a wall, a curved wall, a corner off the grid, an inexact area) turns the command back in the search's own words; not enclosed and redundant are what became of the room, never refusals.
- **The generic commands refuse a room** and name the command to use; a room whose walls change is built again by regeneration, never by a command.
- **The page finds no boundary and works out no area.** The server's `/filled/<room>` carries the loops and the room's state and measures in the document's units; the page draws a light fill with a tag at the room's point (name, number, then area, or its state in words) and refills every room when an entry changes anything but rooms. A room is picked, not dragged: its point moves by `room.move`.
