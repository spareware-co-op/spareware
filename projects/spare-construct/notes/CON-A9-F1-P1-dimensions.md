# CON-A9-F1-P1: Linear dimensions are elements bound to references; the value is worked out, never stored

**Status, 2026-11-27:** Decided for the build (piece A81). The crate `construct-dimensions` follows it; the stored form is in its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-dimensions/FORMAT.md). The first half is the data and the commands; the second is the page tool, the drawing in the plan and the section in the two-browser test.

## The question

A dimension that is a drawn number goes wrong the moment the model moves. The part's own words ask for "dimensions bound to exact references" that "update when the model moves", and the plan job (7.5) asks that a reference that is deleted is reported by name rather than a wrong number shown.

## The decisions

- **A dimension is an element record of the dimensions category, with no shape.** As a view and a sheet are: records are the truth (main's ruling 7), nothing is built from it, `has_shape` is false for its kind, so regeneration and packages pass it over, and the kind table gives `dimension` its category. It lives in one view, today a floor plan or a ceiling plan.
- **Its references are the model's own references.** construct-model's `Reference` already says "part of another element, by persistent name"; a dimension's entries are of kind `dimension.reference`, their name the part (`wall.face.exterior`, `wall.line`, `grid.line`, `level`, `centre`, `edge.start`, `edge.end`). Nothing new is stored beside them. The parts are named by what they are, so a door that moves along its wall keeps its dimension.
- **The value is never stored.** Each segment is worked out from where its two references are, every time. Where a reference is, is asked of the model behind a trait of ours (`Locator`), so the same tests judge any model: a point is read at the foot of the perpendicular to the offset line, a line where it meets it. The server reads the document's records for the answer (a layered wall's filled recipe and its face offsets, a grid's recipe, a hosted element's offset along its wall and its opening's width, a family's solved opening), at the log's head.
- **The arithmetic is exact.** A position along the offset line is a fraction of whole numbers; the distance between two is the square root of a fraction. Whole numbers of any size (`num-bigint`, MIT OR Apache-2.0, pinned) and construct-params' own `Ratio` hold them, and the nearest whole grid step is taken once, at the end; each segment says whether that step is the true value. Horizontal and vertical dimensions between references on whole steps are always exact. A centre that falls on half a step is the step above (a bound of 78 nm): the one place a locus is rounded.
- **Layout is data too.** A horizontal dimension stores the y of its offset line, a vertical one the x, an aligned one a point and a direction. Nothing moves the line when a reference moves: a person moves it (`dimension.move`).
- **A reference that cannot be used is a warning, in the shape construct-warnings uses.** Kind (`dimension.reference_missing`, `dimension.reference_parallel`, `dimension.out_of_range`), severity, the elements (the dimension, then the one referenced) and a sentence naming the reference by position. The segments it ends have no value; the others keep theirs. construct-warnings (A77) is not on main, so the crate repeats its shape field for field and converts one to one when both are there (the header of `warning.rs`).
- **Equality ("EQ") is stored as data and not enforced.** Groups of segments that are to be equal are kept, and the measurement reports whether each group is equal now by the exact values. Enforcing them means moving elements, which needs the solver on elements; the part's words say "equality", not "driven by". While equalities are stored, references are not added or removed (the numbers of the segments would change meaning).
- **Display text is construct-params'.** The value formatted with the person's `Units` through `Definition::format`. The text's size waits for job 7.4: what it will answer (the size of a piece of text) is behind `TextSize`. Until then the plan draws with `PlainText`, one default height (250 mm in a view at 1:100, which the page scales to the plan's scale as it does every text) and a plain width for each character, so a label's `fits` is a plain estimate, not a font's measure; `Unmeasured` still answers where nothing is known.
- **Commands, through the log.** `dimension.create`, `dimension.reference.add`, `dimension.reference.remove`, `dimension.move`, `dimension.equal`, `dimension.delete`: one undo step each, claims on the piece written, refusals in the crates' words, a change that changes nothing writes nothing. The generic element commands are refused for a dimension, naming the command to use. A view that holds dimensions is not deleted. An undo that would leave a dimension in a view that is not there is turned back. Deleting an element a dimension names is not blocked.
- **A read-only view.** `GET .../views/{view}/dimensions` lists a view's dimensions with each segment's value (stored length text and the person's units), its witness lines, its stored equalities and its warnings, as of the log's head.

## Why

- Binding to the part by its name, and reading its place each time, is what lets a dimension follow a moving grid or a door sliding along its wall, and report a deleted wall instead of showing a stale number.
- Exact whole-number arithmetic means a dimension agrees with the kernel to the step; the rounding is in one place and flagged.
- A trait for the model's answer means the exact kernel, a section's frame (job 8.1) and a text engine (job 7.4) each replace one place and no caller.

## Not decided here

Spot levels, coordinates and slopes (A9-F1-P2, job 14.12), auto dimensions (P3, job 14.13), dimensions in sections and elevations, where a level has a place (a section's frame is job 8.1's), and keeping an equality while a reference moves later (the solver on elements). Piece A89 below took the rest.

## The page (second half)

The dimension tool (Annotate, Dimension) picks references by clicking them in the plan: a wall's face or reference line (whichever is nearest the click across the wall), a grid line, a door's, window's or opening's centre or edge (by where along it the click is), then an empty place for the offset line. A dimension belongs to the open view. The page works out no value: it asks the server for the open view's dimensions again whenever the log changes and draws the offset line, the witness lines, a tick at each and each segment's text as the server gave them. A picked dimension has its line dragged (one `dimension.move`, drawn where it was put until the server answers), references added and removed, its segments held equal and Delete, in the properties palette; a deleted or unusable reference is the server's warning, in its words. One dimension with no usable witness is listed in the corner of the plan so it can still be picked and deleted.

## Every style an architect uses (piece A89, 2026-11-28)

**Status:** Decided for the build (piece A89, plan job 14.11). The crate, its FORMAT.md and construct-api's `cloud::dimensions` follow it.

- **Every kind is a layout of the same record.** Angular (`first` and `second` rays and an arc `radius`), radial and diameter (a `toward` direction), arc length (an `offset` outside the arc), and an ordinate dimension (any offset line, marked `@dimension.ordinate`). A linear dimension of piece A81 writes none of the new keys, so its bytes are unchanged; what each kind must reference (two straight lines, one curve) is checked when it is made and reported by name when it stops being so.
- **A curve is a type of ours.** `Locus::Arc(Curve)`, a centre, a radius and a sweep (a whole circle, or anticlockwise between two exact angles). A curved grid line is one today; a curved wall (job 14.4) answers with one under the same feature names, and a line dimension to a curve says it has no straight line to measure to.
- **Angles are exact where they can be, and bounded where they cannot (main's exact-angle rule, as in piece A80).** An angle between directions of whole steps is a whole number of angle units only at a multiple of 45 degrees (Niven's theorem): those are found exactly from the whole-number cross and dot products and marked exact. Every other angle, and every arc length (pi times a whole number), is worked in whole numbers of any size (Euler's arctangent series, Machin's pi, Taylor's sine and cosine, 128 bits after the point) and rounded once, to within half a unit and 2^-80 of one (half a step and 2^-70 of one for a length). The bound is proved in `src/trig.rs` and pinned against values worked out by other methods (Gauss-Legendre pi, halving arctangents, decimal at 90 digits). No float anywhere: the reason the series are our own is that no permissively licensed library gives an arctangent as a whole number with a proved bound.
- **A place is turned into data once.** A person puts an angle, a radius or an arc length by clicking; the command works out which of the four angles holds the place and its distance, the direction from the centre, or the offset, and stores that. The click is not stored, so a dimension never flips to another angle when the walls move.
- **Equality is made by moving the references, in one step, through the log (`dimension.equalize`).** Each group's run keeps its two ends; segments outside the group keep their lengths; the group's segments share the rest, each reference at the nearest step of its exact place (equal to within one step where a length does not divide). Each element moves as its own commands move it and is checked by the same rules: a layered wall's line (exact), the old box wall's placement (whole millimetres, so a part of one is refused by name), a grid by construct-grids, a door, window or opening along its wall with the builder's fit. Another person's claim on an element refuses the whole command, naming the reference. Every group is measured again as written and must hold. Moving a reference afterwards moves nothing else; the listing reports the equality as not holding until it is made again (keeping it on every move needs the solver on elements).
- **Why a command of its own and not `dimension.equal`.** Piece A81's `dimension.equal` stores groups as data and its tests pin that nothing moves; it stays so (a check that segments are equal, reported, is a use of its own), and `dimension.equalize` is the command that makes them so. The page's "Make equal" uses `dimension.equalize`; "Let them differ" clears with `dimension.equal`.
- **Styles are project data.** Two built-in styles (ticks, arrows), never records, never changed; a project's own are records of the dimensions category with no shape, made, changed and deleted by their commands, names unique case aside. A style holds the marker, where the text sits, its own length and angle formats (construct-params' `Format`; none means the project's), the witness gap and extension and the text height as printed on paper, and a centre mark for radii and diameters. A dimension names its style; the first built-in one is named by naming none, so a record has one spelling. Deleting a style a dimension is in is refused, and an undo that would leave a dimension in a style that is not there is turned back.
- **Overrides change what is shown, never the value.** A text in place of the value, a prefix and a suffix, each one line; they are the dimension's (a string's every segment shows them). The listing gives what is shown and, always, the true value beside it, so the page shows it when asked.
- **Kept open for jobs 14.12 and 14.13.** Spot levels and coordinates add a layout (or a kind) and read references and styles as these do; auto dimensions make linear strings with `dimension.create` and may make them equal with `dimension.equalize`.
