# CON-A3-F4-P1: the modify tools, exact on the length grid

Part CON-A3-F4-P1 (modify tools) of [FEATURES.md](../FEATURES.md), first half: move, copy, rotate, mirror and array. Piece A80 (job 10.2). Status: built in `construct-modify` (the motions, each kind's record, the selection) and `construct-api` (`src/cloud/modify.rs`: `modify.move`, `modify.copy`, `modify.rotate`, `modify.mirror`, `modify.array`). The command form is in [construct-model's FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-model/FORMAT.md), "Commands" and "Modify". The page's tools are the second half.

## The question

Every stored coordinate is a construct-params `Length`, a whole number of the kernel's grid steps (1/6,400 mm). A move by a whole number of steps keeps every point on the grid, but a turn or a mirror in general does not. Which turns and mirrors can be offered exactly, what is refused and in what words, how does each kind of element take a motion, what happens to what an element hosts, and how are copies named, so that a group of elements (job 10.2b) can reuse the same operations?

## Decisions

1. **A motion is whole numbers.** A motion of the plan is `p -> (M p + t) / d` with a whole 2 by 2 matrix `M`, a whole vector `t` and a whole `d` above zero, in lowest terms (`construct_modify::Motion`). Applied to a grid point it gives a grid point exactly or says it would not; nothing is rounded. Motions compose exactly (`then`), so a turn of a copy of a turned copy, or a group inside a group, is one motion.
2. **Turns by an angle are quarter turns.** An angle a person types is a whole number of construct-params' angle units (1/9,000,000 of a degree), so a rational number of degrees. A turn about a centre `c` that takes a grid point `p` other than `c` to a grid point `q` has a rational cosine and sine: with `u = p - c` and `v = q - c`, whole vectors of equal length, `cos = (u . v) / |u|^2` and `sin = (u x v) / |u|^2`. By Niven's theorem (I. Niven, *Irrational Numbers*, 1956) the only rational values the sine of a rational number of degrees takes are 0, ±1/2 and ±1, and the same holds for the cosine; both rational with `cos^2 + sin^2 = 1` leaves only the quarter turns (±1/2 would need the other to be the square root of 3/4). So **a quarter turn always lands, and every other angle in degrees moves every point but the centre off the grid**. It is refused, naming the angle and the quarter turns either side of it (`A turn of 30° does not land on the length grid: only a whole number of quarter turns does, and the nearest are 0° and 90°. ...`), never rounded to the nearest. The rule is proved, not sampled: no list of "safe" angles is kept.
3. **Other angles by two directions.** The angles that do land points are those whose cosine and sine are rational, which no angle in degrees is but a turn from one whole direction to another can be: the turn taking `u` to `v` has cosine `(u . v) / (|u| |v|)` and sine `(u x v) / (|u| |v|)`, rational exactly when `|u| |v|` is a whole number (each direction is reduced to lowest terms first, and `|u|^2 |v|^2` is a square exactly when both `|u|^2 / g` and `|v|^2 / g` are, `g` their greatest common divisor, so no number past 128 bits is needed). That is the turn a person makes to line one thing up with another at a 3:4:5 slope. Each point then lands or not by plain division and is refused naming the element when it does not (`W-1 would not land on the length grid: ...`). `modify.rotate` takes `angle`, or `from` and `to`.
4. **Mirrors about a line through two grid points.** The mirror about a line of direction `(a, b)` in lowest terms has the rational matrix `(a^2 - b^2, 2ab; 2ab, b^2 - a^2) / (a^2 + b^2)`. For a line along x or y or at 45 degrees to them it is whole, so it always lands; any other line lands the points the division lets through, each other refused by name.
5. **Each kind is moved by the crate that owns its form** (`construct_modify::kinds`), so a moved record is the record of one drawn there and is read by the same reader: a layered wall's `start` and `end`; a floor's boundary and a roof's footprint corner by corner, each edge keeping its ID (so its side faces keep their names), checked again by construct-slabs and construct-roofs (a roof's footprint turned off the axes with an overhang is refused in construct-roofs' words); a room's point; a shaft's and a floor opening's outline; a grid's or reference plane's geometry, an arc only by the eight motions of a square so its angles stay whole angle units (a mirror runs it the other way, so its ends change places); a block's box by the same eight, in whole millimetres; an older box wall or a door on no host, which runs along x or y as drawn, moved only, in whole millimetres. Anything with no shape (a type, a level, a view, a sheet), a workshop part, and any kind not listed is refused by name, pointing to its own commands.
6. **A mirrored wall keeps its exterior where the mirror puts it.** Walking a wall from its start to its end its exterior is on the right; a mirror reverses that, so the mirrored wall's own `wall.flipped` is written as the other of what it read (its own value or the default). Its line is the mirror of its line, so its location line, its layers' order and every offset along it stay as they were: what it hosts needs nothing written.
7. **Hosted elements follow their host, their records unchanged** (FORMAT.md, "Hosted elements": where one sits is read from its host). Two things in a hosted record name what the host's motion changes: a box door and a wall opening name the axis their wall runs along (`axis`), which a quarter turn, or a mirror at 45 degrees, takes from x to y; and a placed family is seated turned and never mirrored, so the mirror image of a door hinged on the left is the door hinged on the right. A family's hand is any parameter each instance holds that is a choice of exactly `left` and `right` (the library's single swing door's `swing`); a mirror writes the other of what it holds. A family that is handed but declares no hand (the library's sliding window) keeps its sash where it is; giving it a hand parameter is the family's change, not this part's. A floor opening's outline is in its floor's plan, so it is moved by the floor's motion.
8. **A hosted element moved alone** slides along its host by the part of the move along it, or is refused by name when any part is across it (`D-1 is hosted on W-1: alone it moves only along W-1, ...`); turned or mirrored alone it is refused by name; one on a host at an angle is refused by name (a hosted element sits only on a wall along x or y today). An older door hosted by its position is refused by name, pointing to `hosted.move`, which gives it its offsets.
9. **Copies.** A copy is the same motion applied to a copy of each element, hosted elements included. Its ID is made from the key its command names, `<copy>.<n>`, `n` counting the copied elements from 1 in the order of their IDs; an array's copy `k` is `<copy>.<k>.<n>`. The IDs do not grow when a copy is copied, and a person or an agent can say in advance what they will be. A copy keeps its type, parameters and name, except where a name must stay unique: a grid's or a named reference plane's is the next by construct-grids' rule, a room's number the next free one. A hosted element copied with its host is hosted on the host's copy; one copied alone stays on its host. An array of `count` is `count - 1` copies; a linear one by a spacing, or by a span split into equal steps (refused naming the nearest counts that split it on the grid); a radial one by an angle or a sweep, whole quarter turns as any turn by an angle, refused when its copies would come round onto the originals. Each copy is a plain element: an associated array, kept together and edited as one, is a later part of CON-A3-F4.
10. **One entry, one step, checked as built.** Each command is one entry in the one ordered log, takes the claim on every piece it rewrites (another person's claim refuses it, as any edit), and is one undo step. Every record written is filled by construct-regen's `recipe_at` and read by the builder of the document's edition (`face_names`; a room's loop by `found_at`), grids and planes are read again with their names, and every host moved or copied with what it hosts, or along which an element moved, is asked whether everything on it fits by the kernel builder's rules (`kernel::fits`), a refusal in their words. A hosted element the command writes holds the placement that fit gives it.

## Fault found on the way

A host's fit (`construct-api`'s `hosting::fit`, which `family.place` and `family.move` ask too) asked the kernel builder's rules of each placed family as stored, unturned, while the regenerator builds it turned to face out of its side (`construct-regen`'s `hosting::seat`). On a wall along x the two agree; on a wall along y the unturned family runs across the wall, and the fit refused it (`family F-1: it is turned across its wall's axis`) though it builds. The fit now asks each element as its host's builder is handed it, through `seat`, so what is refused and what is built cannot differ (a test places doors on a wall along y).

## For job 10.2b (groups)

`construct_modify::plan` takes a document's records, a selection and a `Request` (`Move(Motion)` or `Copy(Copies)`) and gives the records to write, pure and the same on every machine; `Motion::then` composes motions exactly, so a group's copy placed at a motion, and a group inside it at another, is one motion applied to the type's members. What reads the document beyond a record comes through the `Sense` trait (a wall's side, a family's hands). A group's own copy IDs should follow decision 9's form with the group instance's key.

## Risks

- A selection whose copies would exceed 10,000 records is refused rather than written in parts.
- A turn between two directions whose lengths multiply to a whole number lands only points the division lets through; most points of a model drawn to 0.01 mm do not (a hypotenuse is odd with prime factors of the form 4k + 1, and 0.01 mm is 64 steps), while a model drawn in whole millimetres lands under every turn whose hypotenuse divides 25 (3:4:5, 7:24:25). The refusal names the element; nothing is rounded.
