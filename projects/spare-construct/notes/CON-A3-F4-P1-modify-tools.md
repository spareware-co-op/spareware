# CON-A3-F4-P1: the modify tools, any angle on the length grid

Part CON-A3-F4-P1 (modify tools) of [FEATURES.md](../FEATURES.md), first half: move, copy, rotate, mirror and array. Piece A80 (job 10.2). Status: built in `construct-modify` (the motions, each kind's record, the selection) and `construct-api` (`src/cloud/modify.rs`: `modify.move`, `modify.copy`, `modify.rotate`, `modify.mirror`, `modify.array`). The command form is in [construct-model's FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-model/FORMAT.md), "Commands" and "Modify". The page's tools are the second half. Decisions 2 to 4 and 9 are main's ruling of 28 Nov, which replaced the first rule (only quarter turns by an angle, everything else refused if it fell between steps): a person rotates by any angle and makes radial arrays of any count, as a building-modelling program is expected to.

## The question

Every stored coordinate is a construct-params `Length`, a whole number of the kernel's grid steps (1/6,400 mm). A move by a whole number of steps keeps every point on the grid, but a turn or a mirror in general does not, and a turn by most angles has no exact fraction at all. How is a turn by any angle worked out with no floating point in anything stored or compared, how far may a point land from where the exact turn puts it, how do elements that share a point or host one another stay together through that, what is still refused, how does each kind of element take a motion, and how are copies named, so that a group of elements (job 10.2b) can reuse the same operations?

## Decisions

1. **A motion is whole numbers, rounded once.** A motion of the plan is `p -> (M p + t) / d` with a whole 2 by 2 matrix `M`, a whole vector `t` and a whole `d` above zero, in lowest terms, on whole numbers of any size (num-bigint, MIT OR Apache-2.0), so nothing overflows (`construct_modify::Motion`). Motions compose exactly (`then`), so a turn of a copy of a turned copy, or a group inside a group, is one motion. Applied to a grid point it gives the exact fraction, and each coordinate is rounded once to the nearest grid step, a half away from zero, as construct-dimensions rounds a point. A point the fraction puts on the grid is given exactly.
2. **Any angle.** An angle a person types is a whole number of construct-params' angle units (1/9,000,000 of a degree). A whole number of quarter turns is held exactly. By Niven's theorem (I. Niven, *Irrational Numbers*, 1956) no other rational number of degrees has a rational cosine and sine, so any other angle has no exact fraction; it is held as the true rotation nearest it: the half-tangent `t = tan(θ/2)` is worked out to 2^-160 on whole numbers (pi by Machin's formula, the sine and cosine by their series, with 32 bits more than given; `construct_modify::trig`, whose header proves the bound), and the turn held is the one whose cosine and sine are `(1 - t^2) / (1 + t^2)` and `2t / (1 + t^2)`, fractions whose squares add to exactly one. So what is held is a rotation exactly, and its angle is within 2^-159 of a radian of the angle asked. The angle is first taken to within an eighth of a turn of the nearest quarter turn, alike for an angle and its opposite, so a turn and the turn back by the same angle are exactly no turn.
3. **The bound.** For any point an `i64` holds, the turn held puts it within 2^-94 of a step of where the exact turn puts it (the distance from the centre, under 2^64.5 steps, times the angle's error). Rounded once, **each coordinate is within half a step of the exact turn, and that 2^-94 of a step**: half a step is 1/12,800 mm, under 0.0001 mm, a hundred times inside the model's tolerance of 0.01 mm. Pinned in `construct-modify`'s `tests/exact.rs`: at 30, 45 and 60 degrees, where the exact turn is known in square roots of 2 and 3, every point of a property test (centres and points out to 2^38 steps, 43 km) is checked on whole numbers, by squaring, to be within half a step of it; and points at 37.5, 15, -37.5 and 123.456789 degrees, one angle unit, a seventh of 100 and of 360 degrees, out to 100 km, are pinned to the steps worked out apart from this crate (Python's decimal module at 90 digits, none within 0.04 of a step of a tie). The half-tangents of 22.5 and 15 degrees are checked against `sqrt 2 - 1` and `2 - sqrt 3` to half their last bit.
4. **Turns between two directions and mirrors.** The turn taking a direction `u` to a direction `v` has cosine `(u . v) / (|u| |v|)` and sine `(u x v) / (|u| |v|)`, exact when `|u| |v|` is a whole number (each direction in lowest terms first; that is the turn a person makes to line one thing up with another at a 3:4:5 slope), and otherwise held as decision 2 holds it, from its half-tangent `(u x v) / (|u| |v| + u . v)`, the root worked out to 2^-192 (past a quarter turn either way, a half turn and the turn from `-u`). The mirror about the line through two points, of direction `(a, b)` in lowest terms, is `(a^2 - b^2, 2ab; 2ab, b^2 - a^2) / (a^2 + b^2)`, always exact; along x or y or at 45 degrees every point lands, and any other is rounded once. `modify.rotate` takes `angle`, or `from` and `to`.
5. **Consistent through the rounding.** Every point is rounded from where it is, by the one motion of the command, never from another rounded point: a point two elements share lands on one point, so walls joined at an end stay joined (their shared end is one point, rounded once), and a corner a floor shares with an opening drawn on it stays shared. What a wall hosts sits at its offsets along it and up it (FORMAT.md, "Hosted elements"), so it follows its host with its record unchanged and stays exactly on it however the host's ends are rounded; it is never placed from separately rounded corners. A turn and its way back are exactly no turn, but a point rounded between them was rounded: back by the same angle, a point may be a step from where it began, and the tests say so (`a_turn_and_its_way_back_are_no_turn_but_the_points_were_rounded`; the server's `walls_turn_by_any_angle_and_stay_joined`, where two walls' shared corner comes back one step, 0.00016 mm, from where it was, still shared).
6. **Each kind is moved by the crate that owns its form** (`construct_modify::kinds`), so a moved record is the record of one drawn there and is read by the same reader: a layered wall's `start` and `end`; a floor's boundary and a roof's footprint corner by corner, each edge keeping its ID (so its side faces keep their names), checked again by construct-slabs and construct-roofs (a roof's footprint turned off the axes with an overhang is refused in construct-roofs' words); a room's point; a shaft's and a floor opening's outline; a grid's or reference plane's geometry, an arc's centre a point and its angles by the whole angle units the motion turns them by (any turn by an angle, a radial array's copy, the eight motions of a square; a mirror runs it the other way, so its ends change places); a block's box by the eight motions of a square, in whole millimetres; an older box wall or a door on no host, which runs along x or y as drawn, moved only, in whole millimetres. Anything with no shape (a type, a level, a view, a sheet), a workshop part, and any kind not listed is refused by name, pointing to its own commands.
7. **A mirrored wall keeps its exterior where the mirror puts it.** Walking a wall from its start to its end its exterior is on the right; a mirror reverses that, so the mirrored wall's own `wall.flipped` is written as the other of what it read (its own value or the default). Its line is the mirror of its line, so its location line, its layers' order and every offset along it stay as they were: what it hosts needs nothing written.
8. **Hosted elements follow their host, their records unchanged** (decision 5). Two things in a hosted record name what the host's motion changes: a box door and a wall opening name the axis their wall runs along (`axis`), which a quarter turn, or a mirror at 45 degrees, takes from x to y; and a placed family is seated turned and never mirrored, so the mirror image of a door hinged on the left is the door hinged on the right. A family's hand is any parameter each instance holds that is a choice of exactly `left` and `right` (the library's single swing door's `swing`); a mirror writes the other of what it holds. A family that is handed but declares no hand (the library's sliding window) keeps its sash where it is; giving it a hand parameter is the family's change, not this part's. A floor opening's outline is in its floor's plan, so it is moved by the floor's motion. A hosted element moved alone slides along its host by the part of the move along it, or is refused by name when any part is across it (`D-1 is hosted on W-1: alone it moves only along W-1, ...`); turned or mirrored alone it is refused by name; one on a host at an angle is refused by name. An older door hosted by its position is refused by name, pointing to `hosted.move`, which gives it its offsets.
9. **Copies and arrays.** A copy is the same motion applied to a copy of each element, hosted elements included. Its ID is made from the key its command names, `<copy>.<n>`, `n` counting the copied elements from 1 in the order of their IDs; an array's copy `k` is `<copy>.<k>.<n>`. The IDs do not grow when a copy is copied, and a person or an agent can say in advance what they will be. A copy keeps its type, parameters and name, except where a name must stay unique: a grid's or a named reference plane's is the next by construct-grids' rule, a room's number the next free one. A hosted element copied with its host is hosted on the host's copy; one copied alone stays on its host. An array of `count` is `count - 1` copies, **each worked out from the originals, never from the copy before it**, so each copy's points are rounded once and nothing builds up: a linear one by a spacing (copy `k` exactly `k` spacings on) or by a span (copy `k` the nearest step to `k / (count - 1)` of it); a radial one of any count, by an angle between neighbours (copy `k` the turn by `k` times it) or a sweep to the last (copy `k` the turn by `k / (count - 1)` of it, in whole angle units or not; a sweep of exactly a full turn spreads the count evenly round). A copy that comes to a quarter turn lands exactly however many steps came before it. Copies that would come round onto the originals are refused, naming how many fit. Each copy is a plain element: an associated array, kept together and edited as one, is a later part of CON-A3-F4.
10. **Refused by name only what cannot be built.** A point past the model's limit (`2^40 - 1` steps, about 171.8 km from the origin along x or y, the kernel's grid; `PastLimit`, naming the element); a block turned off the eight motions of a square, or an element held in whole millimetres moved between two (their stored form holds nothing else); an arc by a turn between two directions or a mirror about another line (its angles are whole angle units, and those motions turn them by none); and what the builders refuse of the result, in their words (decision 11). A point between two steps is no refusal.
11. **One entry, one step, checked as built.** Each command is one entry in the one ordered log, takes the claim on every piece it rewrites (another person's claim refuses it, as any edit), and is one undo step. Every record written is filled by construct-regen's `recipe_at` and read by the builder of the document's edition (`face_names`; a room's loop by `found_at`), grids and planes are read again with their names, and every host moved or copied with what it hosts, or along which an element moved, is asked whether everything on it fits by the kernel builder's rules (`kernel::fits`), a refusal in their words. A hosted element the command writes holds the placement that fit gives it. Today the builder hosts a door, a placed family or a wall opening only on a wall along x or y, so a wall that hosts one is refused, in the builder's words (`opening O-1: W-1 runs at an angle: ...`), when it is turned off the axes; a wall that hosts nothing turns by any angle.

## Fault found on the way

A host's fit (`construct-api`'s `hosting::fit`, which `family.place` and `family.move` ask too) asked the kernel builder's rules of each placed family as stored, unturned, while the regenerator builds it turned to face out of its side (`construct-regen`'s `hosting::seat`). On a wall along x the two agree; on a wall along y the unturned family runs across the wall, and the fit refused it (`family F-1: it is turned across its wall's axis`) though it builds. The fit now asks each element as its host's builder is handed it, through `seat`, so what is refused and what is built cannot differ (a test places doors on a wall along y).

## For job 10.2b (groups)

`construct_modify::plan` takes a document's records, a selection and a `Request` (`Move(Motion)` or `Copy(Copies)`) and gives the records to write, pure and the same on every machine; `Motion::then` composes motions exactly, so a group's copy placed at a motion, and a group inside it at another, is one motion applied to the type's members, rounded once (each turn by an angle in the chain adds its 2^-94 of a step to decision 3's bound). What reads the document beyond a record comes through the `Sense` trait (a wall's side, a family's hands). A group's own copy IDs should follow decision 9's form with the group instance's key.

## Risks

- A selection whose copies would exceed 10,000 records is refused rather than written in parts.
- Turned off the axes, a wall along x or y comes back by the same angle within a step of where it was, so it may then run one step off its axis: in plan that is under 0.0002 mm, but the builder reads it as a wall at an angle. Turning it back by a quarter turn, or drawing it again, puts it on the axis exactly.
- A tie (a point exactly half a step from two) can only come from an exact fraction (a 30 degree turn of a point straight along its axis from the centre, say): the turn held there is within 2^-94 of a step of the tie, so which way it goes is fixed, the same on every machine, but not chosen by rule.
