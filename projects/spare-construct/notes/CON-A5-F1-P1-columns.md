# CON-A5-F1-P1: Architectural columns on grids, by level and height

**Status, 2026-11-28:** Decided for the build (piece A87, job 14.7, the data and commands half). The crate `construct-columns` follows it and its [FORMAT.md](../crates/construct-columns/FORMAT.md) is the stored form; construct-regen's `column_plan` reads the filled recipe for both builders, its `columns` fills a column's section from its type and its position from its grid lines, and its `kernel` module's child `column` builds the column through the engine seam. The commands (`column.*`, in construct-api's `cloud/columns.rs`) and the server's read-only view (`GET .../columns`) are in. The page tool, plan and 3D drawing, the two-browser section and the package rebuild are the second half (a later lane). Joining a column into the walls it touches waits for wall joins (piece A60).

## The question

A column is a vertical member that stands at a point, or where two grid lines cross and then stays there when a grid moves, from a base level to a top level (or for a height), of a type that holds its section. A real architect's sample model held 118 of them. Job 14.7 had to settle where a column lives in the code, what its record holds, how it follows grids and levels, how a round or a turned section is built today, and how many are placed at once.

## The decisions

- **A crate of its own, not a kind of the families crate.** A family recipe takes straight lines only in its profiles, hosts on a wall, a floor or a face, and has no way to tie to a level or a grid; a column needs a base and a top at levels, a position on two grids and a type that holds a section. A column is the floor's shape: an element kind of its own, a type with a system block, a recipe filled from its type, its levels and the datums it reads. The families crate stays for what is placed into a host.
- **The record:** kind `column`, the columns category (1100), its type under `@type`, its base tie (`@level`, `@level.offset`) and, for a top at a level, its top tie (`@top.level`, `@top.offset`), as a wall's top is tied. Its recipe `construct/column` holds `at`, `turn`, `section` (`{"type":"section"}`, read from the type) and `height` (`{"tie":"top"}` or a stored length). Moving a level moves the base and the top, and moving a grid moves the column, by regeneration alone: no column record changes.
- **A position on grids is two grid IDs and which crossing** (counted from 0 in order of x then y). Where the two lines cross is construct-grids' `crossings`, so a column is where the datums say, exact where both are straight and on a step. The regenerator lists the two grids in the column's `reads` row from its own recipe (so a column that failed to build is still built again when a grid is made or moved) and, when a grid record changes, builds again the columns that read it, one step deep. A grid deleted, or moved clear of the other, fails the column's build as `BadGrid` in words; it is kept and built again when put right.
- **A type holds the section and the material, as a floor type holds its layers:** the type's system block, owner `column`, one line of JSON in one spelling. "Sizes as type parameters" is this: the sizes are the type's values, every column of the type reads them, and a change to the type changes every column of it. Three types of our own are offered (`column.defaults`): concrete 400 by 400 mm, concrete round 450 mm, timber 140 by 140 mm.
- **Sizes are whole even numbers of grid steps** (so the centre is a step), above zero, at most 5 m; a height is above zero and at most 100 m. A top at the base is `NoHeight`; a top below its base is `TopBelowBase`, saying how far.
- **Round sections are polygons, today.** The engine seam's extrusion takes polygons only, so a round section is a regular polygon of 48 corners on its circle. Its quantities are the polygon's (0.28 per cent under the circle's area), so a schedule reads what is built. The interim register has the row; the kernel's exact sweep of arcs (geom-sweep, chapter 13.4) replaces it under the same tests.
- **The turn follows main's any-angle rule of 28 Nov** (each point from the exact turn, rounded once to the grid step): a column's turn is a stored angle of any value, and each corner is the true turn of its offset from the centre, rounded once, half away from zero. Quarter turns are exact. For other angles the sine and cosine are `libm`'s (as construct-grids takes them), so the bits are the same on every machine, and the bound is stated: half a step plus 2^-24 of a step, for every section up to 5 m. This is interim, with its register row: construct-modify's exact rational turn (piece A80) is the rule's own implementation, but it is not on main and it depends on construct-regen (so the builders cannot depend on it); when it lands, its turn moves into a crate below the builders and replaces `libm` here, under the same test.
- **Quantities are worked out, never stored,** as read-only parameters (`column.height`, `column.area`, `column.volume`, `column.mass`), from the section as built, the height from the levels and the type's material in construct-materials' library.
- **Many at once is one command and one undo step.** `column.place.grids` places a column at each crossing listed; `column.type`, `column.levels`, `column.move`, `column.turn` and `column.delete` take a list. A change to a list is checked as the builders would build every column of it, and nothing is written when any is refused; a column another person is working on turns the whole command back. At most 2,000 columns in one command.
- **A column moved off its grids is let go of them:** `column.move` moves a column from where it stands now; a column on two grids stands at the point it was moved to. To keep a column on its grids, move the grid.
- **Generic commands do not go behind these:** `element.create` of a column, `element.set` of its recipe, `element.move` and the generic type commands on a column type are refused, naming the command to use; `element.delete` stays as for any element.

## Why

- A column whose position, base and top are read when it is built cannot fall out of step with its grids and levels, and an AI or a script reads why a column did not build from its built row.
- Exact lengths in the grid's own unit keep every machine and every replay giving the same bytes, as the stored-units note requires ([CON-A2-F3-P1](CON-A2-F3-P1-stored-units.md)).
- One kind, one type, one command per change, as floors, roofs and rooms are: the same wiring, the same tests, the same words.

## Not decided here

- Joining a column into the walls it touches (the wall's layers wrap or stop at the column), which needs train 10's wall joins (A60). The place: the layered wall builder reads the columns on its level whose rings it crosses, as it reads its joined walls, and cuts its layers back to the ring.
- Slanted columns, columns attached to beams and floors (CON-A5-F1-P1's other words), and section libraries of steel and timber members (CON-A5-F1-P4).
- A grid that is deleted while columns stand on it is today a build failure of those columns, not a refusal of the grid's deletion.
- The modify tools (A80) reading a column's position and turn, so that `modify.move`, `modify.rotate`, `modify.mirror` and `modify.array` take a column: its recipe holds one position and one turn, so each is a rewrite of those two arguments.

## The second half

The Column tool in the page (a click at a point, a pick of grid crossings, a type, a base and a top in the properties palette), the plan drawing of a column (its section, cut at the plan's cut plane, with the structure's line weight) and its 3D shape through the generic shape drawing, a two-browser section (two people place and move columns and a grid), and the package rebuild.
