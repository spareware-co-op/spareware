# Spare Construct app

The browser app, on the [demo's](../demo/index.html) layout. The server sends the demo's own page unchanged and adds these files, so the ribbon, palettes, view tabs and status bar are the real ones and stay in step with it. Everything the demo shows that isn't connected yet says so, rather than showing sample data.

- [connect.js](connect.js): puts live data into the page: sign-in, hubs, projects, folders, the plan and 3D views, the Wall, Door, Family, Opening, Floor opening, Shaft and Component tools, types and parameters, Share, Bridge, Take in and links.
- [view3d.js](view3d.js): what the 3D view draws: one batched mesh per element type, with elements of the same shape sharing one geometry. The renderer of note A04 replaces this file. [view3d-check.html](view3d-check.html) measures it against one mesh per element.
- [pkg](pkg): the same geometry the server builds with, compiled for the browser. `server/scripts/build-shapes.sh` makes it, from `crates/construct-shapes`.
- `pkg-kernel` (not committed): the geometry kernel's builder over solidcore's engine, compiled for the browser, which the page loads for a document whose edition is the kernel's (`construct.kernel`). `server/scripts/build-shapes-kernel.sh` makes it, from `server/shapes-kernel`; only a server built with its `kernel` feature serves it.
- **Workshop**, the discipline for parts, assemblies and drawings, is the demo's own page ([demo/workshop](../demo/workshop)), which the server serves at `/workshop/`. Its parts are built by the geometry kernel, compiled for the browser (`crates/construct-workshop`, made into `demo/workshop/pkg` by `server/scripts/build-workshop.sh`). Its parts and its assembly are elements of the open model, written through the same edit log as walls and doors (`element.create`, `element.set`, `element.delete`), so a part survives a reload and everyone on the model sees it change. They have no place in the plan, so connect.js finds them by kind (`/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/kind/workshop.part`) and hands them to the page as `spare.workshop`. The server's stand-in geometry doesn't build them and records so; the page builds them with the kernel.

- **Parameters**: selecting an element shows its category, its type and its parameters with units, as the server reads them (`/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/parameters/<element>`). An instance value is typed into its field and sent as `element.param` through the same edit log; the server reads the text with its units and turns a wrong unit or kind back with its reason, which the page shows. Types and the parameter table are records with no shape, so the plan leaves them out. The element's type is a list of its category's types in the palette, sent as `element.type`.
- **Types and Project parameters**: two dialogs, opened from the Manage tab or the buttons under an element's parameters. Types lists the types of a category (the selected element's, or one chosen from the server's list of categories) and makes, renames, copies and deletes them and edits their values (`type.create`, `type.rename`, `type.duplicate`, `type.delete`, `type.set`). Project parameters lists every parameter with its kind, who holds its value and the categories it is bound to, defines a parameter and binds it to categories chosen from a checklist (`parameter.define`, `parameter.bind`). The lists come from the server (`/catalog`, `/project-parameters` and `/types` under `/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/`), values are typed and shown as the server formats them, and a refusal is shown in the server's own words beside what caused it. Nothing is checked in the page. Undo and redo cover each command, as they do any edit.
- **Levels**: a dialog opened from the Manage tab lists the model's levels lowest first, with each name, elevation (as the server formats it), whether it is a storey and how many elements are tied to it (`/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/levels`), and makes, renames, moves and deletes them (`level.create`, `level.rename`, `level.elevation`, `level.storey`, `level.delete`); a refusal, such as deleting a level elements are tied to, is the server's reason beside the level. Show makes the plan show that level and ties each wall or door drawn there to it. The floor plans in the browser show Level 1 and Level 2, the levels the old drawings' integers name.
- **Grids and reference planes**: the open model's are found by kind (`/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/kind/grid` and `.../kind/refplane`) and kept apart from the elements; a live-linked model's are found the same way and a shared or consumed package's come in its file. They stand on no level, so every floor plan draws them, under the elements so that a click meant for a wall or a door still reaches it: a grid as a thin chain line, or the chords of its arc, with its name in a bubble at its start, and a plane as a dashed line with its name, in the demo's datum classes; a linked model's are seen and never picked. The **Grid** tool (Architecture tab, Datum) takes two points and sends `grid.create` with no name, so the server gives the next one, after the last grid this person made or renamed. A picked grid shows in the properties palette: its name is typed there (`grid.rename`, or `plane.rename`), a straight grid's ends are dragged by their handles (`grid.reshape`, with both ends), and Delete removes it (`grid.delete`, or `plane.delete`). A refusal, such as a name another grid has, is the server's reason beside the name. Arc grids and planes are made by command for now.

- **Walls**: the Wall tool draws a layered wall between two points, straight along x or y, as one `wall.draw` edit. Its options in the properties palette choose the wall type (the model's wall types, listed with their layers at `/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/wall-types`) and the top (a level, by default the one above the plan's, or a height typed there); the base is the plan's level. Walking from the first point to the second, the exterior is on the right. A model with no wall types gets the server's default ones, sent through the log as `wall.type.create`. A refusal is the server's reason under the options. The plan draws each layer in its material's cut colour and the 3D view in its surface colour (the display's `parts`, on either edition); a material the server doesn't list is drawn in a neutral colour. A door placed on a layered wall is hosted on the side clicked.
- **Floors**: the Floor tool sketches a boundary by clicks in the plan: the floor's edge, then any holes, each closed on its first corner or with Enter; Enter with no loop open, or Make floor, sends one `floor.create` edit, and Escape leaves the tool. Its options in the properties palette choose the floor type (the model's floor types, listed with their layers at `/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/floor-types`) and the offset of its top above the plan's level. A model with no floor types gets the server's default ones, through the log as `floor.defaults`. A refusal is the server's reason under the options, with the boundary it turned back drawn in the plan. The plan draws a floor's edge and holes from its recipe as the server filled it, under the walls, in its top layer's colour seen from above, and the 3D view each layer in its material's colour (the display's `parts`). A selected floor shows its type, its level and offset, its parameters, and apart from them, read-only, what the server works out of it: area, perimeter, thickness, volume and mass, and each layer's volume and mass, in the document's units.
- **Roofs**: the Roof tool, beside Floor, sketches a footprint by clicks in the plan (one loop, closed on its first corner or with Enter); Enter, or Make roof, sends one `roof.create`. Its options in the properties palette choose the roof type (listed with their layers at `/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/roof-types`; a model with none gets the server's default ones through the log as `roof.defaults`), the form (flat, mono-pitch, gable, hip), the pitch (`1:4`, `25%`, `30°`), the edge numbers it rises from (blank for the server's choice), the overhang and the offset of its base. What construct-roofs refuses (two pitches meeting at a hip, a pitch on a footprint that is not right-angled, a hole) is the server's reason in its words under the options, with the footprint it turned back drawn in the plan; the page checks nothing. The plan draws a roof's eaves outline from its recipe as the server filled it, and, where it slopes, the lines of its top face as the server listed them in the filled recipe (`lines`: ridge dashed, hips solid, valleys dotted, verges thin). A hip roof is a form of the same command; a selected roof with two or more edges it rises from shows a pitch for each (`roof.pitch` with `edge`, so an uneven gable is made there). A selected roof shows its type, level, form, pitch and overhang, changed there (`roof.retype`, `roof.form`, `roof.pitch`, `roof.overhang`), what the server works out of it read-only, and Delete removes it (`roof.delete`).

- **Rooms**: the Room tool places a room by a click inside a loop of walls on the plan's level, as one `room.place` edit; its options in the properties palette are the name, the number (the next free one is offered, from `/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/rooms`), and the upper limit (a level, or a height). A refusal is the server's words beside the tool. The plan draws a room as a light fill from its recipe as the server filled it (`/filled/<element>`, with `room`: its state, area, perimeter and volume in units), under the floors, with a tag at its point giving name, number and area; a room that is not enclosed, or is in a loop an earlier room has, shows that in words on its tag and in the palette, never an area. A selected room shows its name, number and upper limit to change, what the server works out of it read-only, and Delete (`room.delete`). A room is not dragged. The 3D view does not draw rooms.

- **Views**: the project browser's Views part lists the model's views (`/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/views`), kept live through the log, under the plans the page always has. A person makes a plan view (a name and a level, `view.create`), and renames (type the new name, Enter, `view.rename`) or deletes (`view.delete`) the open one there; the generic element commands are never used for a view (and `hasShape` leaves view records out of the plan). Opening a floor plan shows that level with the plan as the server draws it from the stored model (`.../views/<view>/plan`: cut lines at their pens and hatches by material): the page draws that picture and cuts nothing itself, asks again a moment after any edit that changes the model or the view, and says in the pane when the server cannot draw it (a server built without the geometry kernel) or leaves an element out. The live elements stay in the plan on top of the picture with no fill of their own, so picking, claims, the Wall, Floor, Door and Grid tools and dragging work as before. **View graphics**: the open view's line also has a Graphics button, a dialog on how a category looks in that view, for what it cuts and for what it sees beyond the cut plane: shown, pen, line pattern and colour, a cut's fill pattern and colour, halftone and transparency, each as the server lists it with where it came from (this view or the project; `/views/<view>/graphics`). A control sends only its own part as `view.graphics.set`, Clear is `view.graphics.clear`, a refusal is the server's words beside the controls, and the rules the view holds are listed with Edit and Clear. Undo and Redo are the log's, and the plan the server draws follows the rules in every browser.
- **Families**: the Family tool, beside the Door tool, lists the library's door and window families and their types (`/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/families`) in the properties palette; a click on a wall sends one `family.place` edit (the family, its type, the wall, the side clicked, how far along the wall), with the values each placed one holds (a window's sill height) typed beside. A selected family shows its type (`family.retype`), its values (`family.set`), its place along its wall (`family.move`, also a drag along the wall) and its side (Flip, `family.flip`), and Delete is `family.delete`; a refusal is the server's words under the fields. In the plan and in 3D it stands where its wall puts it, turned to face out of its side, built from its record by the browser's own geometry; on the kernel's edition the wall is built cut for it. The old box Door tool is still beside it until it is retired. Interim, as the library is (job 7.6).
- **Components** (piece A90): the Component tool, after the Shaft tool, lists the rest of the library (furniture, casework, plumbing fixtures, lighting, planting, generic models) by category, then family, then type, with the values each placed one holds, in the properties palette (the Family tool lists only what cuts a wall; each family says what it is placed on). A click in the plan puts a family that stands on a level at the point clicked on the level the plan shows, turned by the quarter turn chosen (0, 90, 180 or 270 degrees) and raised by the offset typed, one `family.place` edit; a click on a wall hangs a family that hangs against a face (a wall cabinet, a basin), as the Family tool places a door. A selected one that stands shows its turn (`family.turn`), its point (`family.move`, also a drag) and its offset from the level (`family.move`); the rest are as above. Interim: only quarter turns are built (any angle waits for piece A80's rule).

An element here is its recipe. The page builds it through the package its document's edition names and draws what comes back, an outline and a mesh, so it doesn't change when the geometry does. A recipe that reads records (a layered wall's layers from its type, a top tied to a level) is filled by the server, at `/api/projects/<project>/models/<model>/filled/<element>`, as its regenerator fills it; the page builds that, fills it again when a type, a parameter or a level changes, and compares it with the server's bodies only from that point of the log on. A browser that can't build a document's edition draws the server's stored shapes and doesn't edit.

The desktop shell and the wgpu viewport come later, on top of `construct-api`.
- **Openings and shafts**: the Opening tool takes a click on a wall (its side, the middle of the opening; width, height and sill typed in the palette) and sends `opening.place`; the Floor opening and Shaft tools take two opposite corners of a rectangle in the plan (`opening.draw` in the floor under the first corner; `shaft.create` between the two levels chosen in the palette, each with an offset). A selected one shows its size, place or reach in the palette and changes with `opening.size`, `opening.move`, `opening.outline`, `shaft.outline` and `shaft.reach`; Delete sends `opening.delete` or `shaft.delete`. In the plan a wall opening is the rectangle it opens through its wall, a floor opening and a shaft their outlines, and a floor is drawn with the holes the server says cut it (`cut_by` in `/filled/<floor>`); in 3D the wall and the floor are built with them where the package cuts (the kernel's). A refusal is the server's words, beside the tool or the field. A floor's area in the palette is its area after the cut.
