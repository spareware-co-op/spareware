# Name protection: stopping anyone else claiming "Spareware"

*Owner-requested note. Researched and re-checked on 2026-10-19. Not legal advice: a trade mark attorney must confirm every item marked UNVERIFIED. Nothing has been filed, reserved, bought or registered. The owner files and pays. No agent files anything.*

## The answer

Spareware is international. The owner lives in New Zealand, so IPONZ (the New Zealand office) is the natural office to file first. It is also the office that can send a Madrid Protocol application on to WIPO.

**The cheapest sensible way:**

1. **Now:** the owner files the word mark SPAREWARE at IPONZ himself, in 3 classes (9, 35 and 42). It costs NZ$345.00 including GST. He files as an individual and assigns it later, free of charge, to the future legal entity.
2. **Within 6 months of that filing date:** he files one Madrid Protocol application based on it. Word mark, no colour. It names the EU, the US, the UK and Australia. It costs CHF 4,033, charged by WIPO in Swiss francs.
3. **Now:** he buys the key domains and claims the free handles. The first year costs about NZ$250 to NZ$270.

No single registration covers the whole world. A trade mark is national or regional. The NZ filing date plus the 6-month priority window is the cheapest way to hold your place in every other country.

### Step 6. After filing

- Answer any compliance report by its deadline, or the application lapses.
- As of September 2026, IPONZ was examining applications filed 6 to 13 August 2026. A first response takes up to 60 working days instead of the normal 15.
- After acceptance, the mark is advertised. A 3-month opposition window follows. The opponent can extend it by 1 month, or 2 months with consent.
- Registration happens at the later of 3 months after the Journal notice or 6 months after filing. The registration date is deemed to be the filing date. It lasts 10 years, with a 6-month grace period for renewal.
- The research estimates 6 to 8 months from filing to registration for a clean application. That is the researcher's estimate, not an IPONZ figure.
- **The 6-month clock for Madrid starts on the filing date.** My suggestion, not IPONZ advice: file the Madrid application after IPONZ's first response is in, as long as it lands inside the 6 months.

### The logo and "Spareware Co-op"

- **Logo.** The red (#C8102E) circle with a white S is a separate application and costs the per-class fee again. I suggest leaving it for later. The word mark is the core right. In Madrid, a colour mark costs CHF 903 instead of CHF 653.
- **Series.** A red and a black-and-white version can go in one NZ application as a series (Trade Marks Act 2002 s 32(1) and s 51(c); s 5 defines "series"). A series must be cut down to one mark for Madrid.
- **"Spareware Co-op".** "Co-op", "Cooperative" and "Co-operative" are on IPONZ's protected words list, so registering it "may be difficult or prohibited". Leave it out for now. File it later in the name of a registered co-operative company, if one exists.

### Who files now, and how to assign it later

- **File as an individual.** IPONZ accepts individuals, using the full personal name. The Act lets a person claiming to be the owner apply for a mark "used or proposed to be used" (s 32). IPONZ does not investigate ownership at examination.
- **Assign later.** A pending application can be assigned (s 12(1)). A registered mark can be assigned too (s 82), with or without goodwill (s 10(1)(c)).
- **How.** File a change-of-owner request online, with a deed of assignment signed by both parties. IPONZ provides a sample deed. The new owner needs an NZ or Australian address for service. There is no fee. IPONZ's video says an examiner acts within 15 working days.
- **Madrid link.** Assigning the NZ mark does not break the 5-year dependency. The international registration's owner must also be changed at WIPO (CHF 177). Whether the future entity is eligible to hold it under Madrid rules is UNVERIFIED. Ask the attorney.
- **Which entity.** The legal entity is not decided. K01 recommends a structure but is still in review. The assignment works for any entity that is a legal person.
- **UNVERIFIED legal point.** An individual must claim to own the mark and honestly intend to use it in every listed class. Filing 8 classes personally for services a future entity will run could be challenged in opposition or invalidity. Ask the attorney to confirm the ownership and licence-back wording before filing.

## 2. International: the Madrid Protocol

### How it works

1. Base it on the NZ application. The owner and the mark must be the same. The NZ mark must cover every class named overseas.
2. In IPONZ's online case management, choose "Apply for International Registration (NZ Office of Origin)". IPONZ certifies it in about 5 working days and sends it to WIPO. The IPONZ fees page lists no fee for this step.
3. Work out the WIPO fees with WIPO's fee calculator. Pay WIPO directly, in Swiss francs.
4. File within 6 months of the NZ filing date to claim that date in every country. The 6 months comes from the Paris Convention. Each country applies its own law to it.
5. File the word mark with no colour: basic fee CHF 653. With any colour it is CHF 903.
6. Every country named here charges an individual fee, so there is no complementary fee (CHF 100 per country) and no supplementary fee (CHF 100 per class over 3).

### The 5-year dependency

- For 5 years from the international registration date, the whole registration depends on the NZ mark. If the NZ mark is withdrawn, lapses, is renounced, or is finally refused, revoked or invalidated, the international registration is cancelled to the same extent in every country.
- This also applies if the action that leads to it began inside the 5 years. WIPO's Guide uses "central attack" for a challenge to the basic mark, usually by a third party.
- A pending NZ application is riskier as a base, because it can still fail through an IPONZ refusal or an NZ opposition.
- Over-broad NZ claims invite non-use cancellation, which cascades in the same way. Claim only classes you honestly intend to use.
- Assigning the NZ mark to someone else does not break the dependency.
- After 5 years, each country stands alone.
- **Safety net (transformation).** If the registration is cancelled because the NZ base failed, the owner has 3 months from WIPO's recording of the cancellation to file national applications in each country. A country added by later designation keeps its later date. The WIPO Guide covers the dependency on printed pages 161 to 164 and transformation on pages 164 to 165. National fees and attorney fees apply. The amounts were not researched (UNVERIFIED).

### US extras

- The USPTO requires a US-licensed attorney for a foreign owner once it issues a provisional refusal.
- A declaration of use is due between the 5th and 6th anniversaries, with a 6-month grace period. If it is missed, US protection is cancelled. Fee US$325 per class.
- US renewal individual fee: CHF 249 per class.
- A direct USPTO filing costs US$350 per class (US$1,050 for 3), but needs a US-licensed attorney from the start.

Ask the lawyer whether naming products with the house name first (for example "Spareware Ride") is safer than "Spare Ride".

### Where a trade mark lawyer is needed

1. **Before filing.** Settle the classes, the owner-as-individual question and the licence-back wording. Ask whether Spare Labs could object to SPAREWARE. Ask whether "-ware" and the logo are distinctive enough.
2. **Before using or filing** Spare Ride, Spare Office, Spare Work or Spare OS in the EU, UK, Canada or US (Spare Labs).
3. **Before using Spare Food in the US,** or filing it in the EU or UK (The Spare Food Co.).
4. **Before taking** Spare Stay, Spare Rent or Spare Home to Australia, the EU or the US (SpareRoom, SpareSpace Storage, Sparen).
5. **A search for unregistered use.** Registers do not show it. In NZ it can still block a name through passing off or the Fair Trading Act 1986. The same applies in Australia.
6. **Any US refusal.** A US-licensed attorney is required.
